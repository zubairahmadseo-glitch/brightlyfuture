# Verified Sources Rule (user instruction, 30 Oct 2026) — MANDATORY

Applies to EVERY workout plan page and EVERY exercise page on exercisemenu.com: workout structure, sets × reps, rest, progression, muscles worked, form and cues, common mistakes, warm-up, nutrition (pre/post workout, protein, calories), supplements, safety notes, FAQs — every section, named or not.

## Works together with competitor research
This rule never replaces the competitor + keyword research in `BRIEF.md` (top 7 ranking pages; short/mid/long-tail keywords; entities; PAA, related and autocomplete queries; outlines and gaps). Do that research first to decide what the page covers and how it matches search intent; then verify every recommendation here.

## The rule
1. Every recommendation (what to do, how much, how to do it, what to eat) must match the verified sources below. Read the actual abstract / position stand / guideline before writing it.
2. Write it in our own words, in a unique way, in the voice of an experienced coach (Sadia Baloch's coaching approach): practical framing, clear cues, real-world tips. **Experience shapes how we explain, never what is true.** Every claim inside that voice must still be backed by a source here.
3. Never invent personal stories, client results, numbers, studies, quotes or credentials. If a coaching tip has no source behind it (pure technique cue), keep it standard and widely taught (NSCA / ExRx / physio guidance), not a novel claim.
4. Prefer: position stand or meta-analysis > single study > evidence-based coach site. Coach sites give ideas; verify them on PubMed before citing.
5. Check the year: newer position stands override older ones (e.g. ACSM 2026 over ACSM 2009).
6. Cite with working links (PubMed or the organisation's page) in `batch1/content/refs.py`; cite inline next to the claim.

## Tier 1 — research databases
- PubMed — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov (eutils API works from the container)
- PubMed Central (free full text) — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/
- Europe PMC — https://europepmc.org
- Cochrane Library — https://www.cochranelibrary.com
- Google Scholar — https://scholar.google.com (citation counts, finding papers)

## Tier 2 — position stands and official guidelines
- ACSM (sets, reps, frequency, progression; 2026 resistance training position stand) — https://www.acsm.org
- NSCA (strength training, technique, programming; journal JSCR) — https://www.nsca.com
- ISSN (protein, creatine, nutrient timing / pre- and post-workout eating) — https://www.sportsnutritionsociety.org
- WHO physical activity guidelines — https://www.who.int
- US Physical Activity Guidelines — https://health.gov
- Australian Institute of Sport supplement framework — https://www.ais.gov.au
- NIH Office of Dietary Supplements — https://ods.od.nih.gov
- USDA FoodData Central (food protein/calories) — https://fdc.nal.usda.gov

## Tier 2 — journals (highest value studies)
Sports Medicine · British Journal of Sports Medicine · Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise · Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research · Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition · European Journal of Applied Physiology · Journal of Applied Physiology

## Tier 3 — evidence-based coaches/researchers (ideas; verify before citing)
Stronger By Science (Greg Nuckols) · Barbell Medicine (physicians) · StrengthLog · RP Strength (Mike Israetel) · Brad Schoenfeld (lookgreatnaked.com) · Menno Henselmans · Examine.com
Researchers to search on PubMed: Brad Schoenfeld (hypertrophy), Stuart Phillips (protein), Eric Helms (bodybuilding nutrition), Jozo Grgic (rest, caffeine), Michael Zourdos (RPE), Alan Aragon & Jose Antonio (nutrient timing).

## Muscles, anatomy, technique
ExRx.net (muscles worked, exercise library) · Kenhub · TeachMeAnatomy · Physiopedia · NSCA *Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning* (textbook)

## Medical safety (YMYL)
Mayo Clinic · Cleveland Clinic · NHS (nhs.uk) · Harvard Health — for pain, injury, medical conditions, pregnancy warnings. Never give medical advice; refer to a doctor/physio.
