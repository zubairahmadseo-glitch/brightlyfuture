Quick answer

A complete core workout includes one exercise for each core job: a crunch or leg raise, a plank or ab wheel, an anti-rotation press, a side plank or carry, and a back extension. Do 2–3 sets of each, three times a week, and make them harder once they feel easy.

Put core work at the end of your gym sessions on three non-consecutive days. Doing it at the start tires the muscles that keep your trunk stable during squats and deadlifts. Alternate Core A and Core B; on busy days, do the 10-minute version.

What Each Core Exercise Trains

Your core is more than your six-pack muscle. It includes the rectus abdominis, the obliques on your sides, the deep transverse abdominis and the muscles along your spine. Each workout trains all of them by covering these five jobs:

Core jobMain musclesExercises in this plan
Bending forward (flexion)Rectus abdominisCable crunch, reverse crunch, hanging knee raise
Resisting arching (anti-extension)Rectus abdominis, transverse abdominisPlank, ab wheel rollout, dead bug
Resisting twisting (anti-rotation)ObliquesPallof press, bird dog
Resisting side bendingObliques, quadratus lumborumSide plank, suitcase carry
Extending the spineErector spinae, glutesBack extension

Rotation exercises such as the cable wood chop and the medicine ball Russian twist add a sixth job, turning the trunk, and are optional extras.

How to Make Core Exercises Harder

Your abs adapt like any other muscle, so the same plank every week stops working. Progress in this order:

Exercise typeWhen to progressHow
Holds (plank, side plank)You can hold 45–60 sec with good formMove to a harder version (feet elevated, long-lever plank) instead of holding longer
Reps (crunch, leg raise)You hit the top of the rep range on every setAdd weight on the cable stack or straighten your legs
CarriesYou walk 30 m without leaningUse a heavier weight

Will a Core Workout Give You Visible Abs?

Not on its own. Everyone has abdominal muscles; you can see them only when little enough fat covers them. In a controlled study, people who did ab exercises five days a week for six weeks improved their ab endurance but lost no more belly fat than people who didn't train. A core workout makes your abs stronger; overall fat loss through your diet decides when they show.

Core Workout Mistakes

  • Arching your lower back. If your back sags in a plank or lifts off the floor in a dead bug, shorten the lever or the range until you can keep it flat.
  • Pulling on your neck. In crunches, curl your ribs toward your hips; your hands only support your head.
  • Doing only crunches. Crunches train one of the five jobs. A balanced core workout covers all of them.
  • Holding planks for minutes. Long holds mostly train patience. A harder 30–45 second variation does more.

Core Workout FAQ

Can I train my core every day?

Light core work every day is fine for most people, but daily hard sessions don't leave time to recover. Three focused sessions a week is enough to get stronger.

Do squats and deadlifts train my core?

They do, mainly the muscles that keep your trunk stiff and your spine still. They don't train bending forward or resisting side bending much, which is why direct core exercises are still worth doing.

Is a core workout upper or lower body?

Neither. Your core connects the two, so you can add it to the end of either session.

How many reps and sets should I do for abs?

Most people do well with 2–3 sets of 10–15 reps, or 20–45 second holds, per exercise. Once you can do more than 15–20 clean reps, add weight or switch to a harder variation.

Should I feel my lower back during core exercises?

No. Feeling it in your lower back usually means your back is arching. Shorten the lever (bend your knees, plank on your knees) and only progress once your back stays flat. See a physiotherapist if your back hurts.
