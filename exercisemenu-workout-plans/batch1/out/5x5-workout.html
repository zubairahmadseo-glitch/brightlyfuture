Quick answer

The 5x5 workout alternates two sessions three days a week. Workout A is squat, bench press and barbell row; Workout B is squat, overhead press and deadlift. Every lift is 5 sets of 5 except the deadlift (1 set of 5). Add 2.5 kg to upper-body lifts and 5 kg to squats and deadlifts each session.

Use the tabs to see Workout A and Workout B. Starting weights, the session-by-session progression and the stall rules follow.

Workouts alternate, so one week is A-B-A and the next is B-A-B. Keep a rest day between sessions. You squat every session; bench press, row, overhead press and deadlift each come up three times every two weeks.

Workout A and Workout B

Choose a workout. All five sets of five use the same weight. Rest about 3 minutes between sets and up to 5 minutes once the weights get heavy; in trained lifters, rests longer than 2 minutes produced more strength gains in a systematic review.

🔬 Why five sets?

In a 6-month study of untrained men training three times a week, 5 sets per exercise produced bigger strength gains on some lifts and more upper-arm muscle growth than 1 or 3 sets.

Starting Weights

  • Know your 5-rep max? Start at about 50% of it. If you can bench 80 kg for 5, start at 40 kg.
  • New to lifting? Start every lift with the empty bar (20 kg men's bar, 15 kg women's bar).
  • Deadlift: start light, but keep the bar at standard height (about 22 cm off the floor) by using full-size bumper plates or raising the bar on blocks or plates.

Starting light is the point: you get weeks of practice on each lift before the weights become hard.

Linear Progression: Session by Session

If you complete all 5 × 5, add weight next time you do that lift: +5 kg on squat and deadlift, +2.5 kg on bench press, overhead press and row. Example of the first two weeks:

The squat climbs fastest because you squat every session. When 2.5 kg jumps get too big on the overhead press or bench press, use fractional plates (0.25–0.5 kg per side) for jumps of 0.5–1 kg. Warm up for every lift with the empty bar × 10, then about 40%, 60% and 80% of your work weight for 5, 3 and 2 reps; the warm-up set calculator does the maths.

What to Do When You Stall

  1. Miss once: keep the same weight next session.
  2. Miss twice: same weight again, with 5 minutes of rest between sets.
  3. Miss three times in a row: drop that lift by 10% and build back up.
  4. Stall again near the same weight: switch that lift to 3 × 5, then 3 × 3. Once you need 1 × 5, you have outgrown this program.

At that point move to a program that progresses weekly rather than every session, such as our 3-day full body workout for intermediates or the 4-day upper lower split.

5×5 Workout FAQ

Is 5x5 good for building muscle?

Yes, especially for beginners. Heavy compound lifts done for several sets build muscle as well as strength, and adding arm accessories after 6–8 weeks covers the smaller muscles.

Can women do the 5x5 workout?

Yes. The lifts and rules are the same. Women often start with a 15 kg bar and benefit from fractional plates sooner on upper-body lifts.

Can I do 5x5 with dumbbells only?

You can follow the same structure with goblet squats, dumbbell presses, dumbbell rows and dumbbell Romanian deadlifts, but progress stalls sooner because dumbbell jumps are larger and maximum weights are lower.

Can I add cardio on rest days?

Yes. Walking, cycling or swimming at an easy pace is fine. Avoid hard intervals the day before a session so your squats don't suffer.

How long can I keep progressing on 5x5?

It varies with your starting strength, sleep and food. Many lifters progress for a few months before the stall rules move them to an intermediate program.
