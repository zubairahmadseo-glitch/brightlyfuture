Swap = a like-for-like alternative if the equipment is busy or the lift doesn't suit you. Change one exercise at a time and keep it for at least 4 weeks so you can track progress.

🔬 Why A days rest longer

Trained men who rested 3 minutes between sets gained more strength and quad size than those who rested 1 minute in a 2016 study. That is why heavy A-day lifts use 2.5–4 minutes and B-day isolation work uses 60–90 seconds.

Each movement pattern comes up twice a week: once heavy, once with more volume. Push days train chest, shoulders and triceps; pull days train back, rear delts and biceps; leg days train quads, hamstrings, glutes and calves. If six days in a row is too much, run Push A, Pull A, Legs A, rest, then Push B, Pull B, Legs B, rest.

Direct sets count fully and indirect sets count as half, the method that best fit the data in a 2026 dose-response meta-regression. That analysis found more sets keep adding growth, but with diminishing returns, and the 2026 ACSM position stand links hypertrophy with 10 or more sets per muscle per week. Most muscles here sit well above that. If you are not recovering, remove one set from each B-day exercise first.

How to Add Weight: Double Progression

Stay at one weight until every set reaches the top of the rep range with good form, then add the smallest jump available (usually 2.5 kg upper body, 5 kg lower body) and work back up. This is the simplest form of progressive overload.

When an isolation exercise can't go up in weight, add a rep per set first. Log every set so you know what to beat next session; the workout tracking guide shows a simple format.

Deload Weeks

Train hard for 3 weeks, then deload in week 4 and again in week 8. Keep the same days and exercises, cut sets roughly in half, use lighter loads and stop each set with 3–4 reps left. Strength and physique athletes in a 2024 survey deloaded about every 5–6 weeks for roughly a week, so if you still feel fresh in week 4 you can train one more week first. Deload early if your lifts drop two sessions in a row. More detail is in the deload week guide.

Warm-Up

Spend 3 minutes on easy cardio, 3–4 minutes on dynamic moves (arm circles, leg swings, band pull-aparts, bodyweight squats), then do ramp-up sets of your first lift: empty bar × 10, about 50% × 5, about 75% × 3. Our warm-up guide covers each step.

What to Eat on a PPL Split

Eat about 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight daily; muscle gains plateaued near 1.62 g/kg in a meta-analysis of 49 trials. To add size, eat a little above maintenance and aim to gain about 0.25–0.5% of body weight per week, as a review of off-season nutrition recommends. Find your number with the protein calculator.

Common PPL Mistakes

  • Ego lifting. If you have to swing or bounce the weight, it's too heavy for that rep range.
  • Skipping face pulls. Six pressing exercises a week need rear-delt and upper-back work to balance them.
  • Long cardio before lifting. Do hard cardio after lifting or on another day; see cardio before or after weights.
  • Changing the plan every week. Run all 8 weeks before judging it.

Push Pull Legs Workout Plan FAQ

What if I miss a day?

Do the missed workout the next day and shift the rest of the week by one day. Don't combine two workouts into one session.

Do I have to deadlift on pull day?

No. If deadlifts leave your rows weak, move them to the end of Legs A or swap them for rack pulls, which train the back with less leg fatigue.

Can I do push pull legs only 3 days a week?

Yes, as Push, Pull, Legs once a week, alternating A and B workouts each week. Each muscle is then trained once a week, so progress is slower than on the 6-day plan.

Can I add more ab work?

Yes. Add 2–3 sets of planks or ab wheel rollouts at the end of any session. Squats, deadlifts and overhead presses already train your trunk hard.

Can I follow this PPL plan while cutting?

Yes. Keep the weights heavy, keep protein at the upper end of 1.6–2.2 g/kg, and drop one set per exercise on B days if recovery suffers.

When will I see results?

Strength usually rises within the first few weeks. Visible muscle changes often take 6–8 weeks; compare photos and your training log every 4 weeks.
