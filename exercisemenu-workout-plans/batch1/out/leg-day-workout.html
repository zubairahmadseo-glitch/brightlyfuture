Two leg days a week, three or four days apart, let you train each leg muscle twice. Training a muscle twice a week builds more muscle than once a week in a meta-analysis. If you only have time for one leg day, use the beginner workout or alternate the quad and hamstring days from week to week.

🔬 Why squats aren't enough

In a 10-week squat study, full squats grew the quads, glutes and inner thighs, but not the hamstrings or the rectus femoris (the quad that crosses the hip). That's why every plan here includes a Romanian deadlift or a seated leg curl, and the quad day adds a leg extension. For the leg curl, choose the seated machine: it grew the hamstrings more than the lying leg curl in a 2021 study.

For calves, spend most of each rep in the bottom half. Partial reps in the stretched position grew the inner calf more than full reps in a calf training study, so pause at the bottom and don't rush the top.

How Many Sets and Reps on Leg Day?

Each workout has 15–16 hard sets. Use 5–8 reps on heavy squats, 8–12 on most other exercises and 10–15 on isolation and calf work. Rest 2–3 minutes between sets of squats and deadlifts: longer rest built more leg muscle than one-minute rest in a study on rest periods. Running the quad day and hamstring day each week gives your quads about 12 direct sets, your hamstrings 10, your glutes about 16 and your calves 6, in line with the 10 or more weekly sets per muscle the 2026 ACSM position stand links with more growth. If your calves lag, add one set to each calf exercise.

New to leg training? Start with two sets per exercise and add a set every two weeks. Our guide to sets per muscle group explains how to adjust.

How to Progress Your Leg Day

Add weight when you complete the top of the rep range on every set: 2.5–5 kg on squats, Romanian deadlifts and the leg press, and the smallest step available on machines. Track your numbers each session; if your squat stalls for three sessions in a row, take a lighter week. See progressive overload for more options, and our guide to types of squats if you need a different squat variation.

Leg Day Mistakes

  • Cutting squat depth. Half squats build less muscle. Go as deep as you can with a neutral back. If your knees ache, read why your knees hurt when squatting.
  • Skipping hamstrings. Squats and leg presses barely train them. Every leg day needs a hinge or a leg curl.
  • Bouncing calf raises. Pause for a second at the bottom of each rep.
  • Doing cardio-style circuits on leg day. Short rests lower the weight you can use on every set; keep circuits for conditioning days.

Leg Day Workout FAQ

Can I build legs without squats?

Yes. The leg press, Bulgarian split squat and hack squat all train the quads and glutes well. Squats are one good option, not a requirement.

Should I do cardio after leg day?

Light walking or easy cycling is fine. Leave hard intervals or long runs for another day so they don't hurt your next leg session.

How long should leg day take?

About 50–65 minutes including warm-up. Leg exercises need longer rests than upper-body work, so a leg day with 15–16 sets rarely fits into 30 minutes.

How many exercises should I do on leg day?

Four to six is enough. Cover a squat or press, a hinge, a single-leg move and calves; adding more exercises past that usually means lower-quality sets on the ones that matter.

What should I train the day after leg day?

Upper body. Your legs need 48–72 hours before another hard session, but your chest, back and arms are fresh.
