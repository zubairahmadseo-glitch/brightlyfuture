Quick answer

A bro split trains one main muscle group per session: chest Monday, back Tuesday, shoulders Wednesday, legs Thursday and arms Friday. Each muscle gets one hard, high-volume session plus indirect work from the other days. It suits intermediate lifters who can train five days a week.

Many bro split routines online cram a huge number of chest sets into one day and leave hamstrings, rear delts and calves under-trained. Built with the right weekly volume, a bro split workout plan builds muscle as well as other splits.

Below you get the full 5-day schedule, every exercise with sets, reps, rest and effort targets, a weekly volume audit, and the progression and deload rules that keep the plan working past the first month.

What Is a Bro Split Workout Plan?

A bro split is a body-part split: each training day targets one major muscle group (sometimes two small ones) with several exercises from different angles.

The appeal is focus. You can push one muscle hard with 12–16 sets, use machines and cables that suit that muscle, and leave with a strong pump. The trade-off is frequency: each muscle gets one direct session per week.

🔬 What the research says

A 2016 meta-analysis found that training a muscle twice a week produced more growth than once a week. Newer, larger analyses refine that picture. A 2024 meta-analysis of 14 studies found split and full-body routines produced similar strength and size gains when weekly volume was equal, and a 2026 dose-response meta-regression found frequency had little effect on growth once volume was accounted for. So a bro split works, as long as total weekly sets are high enough.

That is why this plan counts sets carefully, including the work each muscle gets on other days.

Who Should Use This 5-Day Bro Split?

This plan fits lifters who have trained consistently for at least 6–12 months and can perform the bench press, squat, row and overhead press with stable technique. Beginners progress faster with full-body training, where each lift is practiced several times a week.

  • You can train five days a week and want sessions under 75 minutes.
  • You enjoy machine and cable work and a muscle-focused training style.
  • Your recovery suits one hard session per muscle, then several days off it.
  • You want a change of stimulus after months of a push pull legs split or upper/lower routine.
💡 Coach's note

If you can only commit to four days, skip this plan. Missing one day of a bro split removes a muscle group for the whole week. Our 4-day workout split trains everything twice and is more forgiving.

If that profile fits, the next step is the weekly layout, because the order of days matters more on a bro split than on most programs.

Pick a day below to see the full session. Each workout opens with a heavy compound lift in the 6–10 rep range, then moves to machine, cable and dumbbell work in the 10–15 range where you can train close to failure with less joint stress.

How to read "Effort": RIR means reps in reserve. RIR 1–2 means you stop each set with one or two good reps left. If the scale is new to you, read the RPE vs RIR guide first.

🔬 Why the rest periods differ

In a study of trained men, 3-minute rests produced more strength and quad growth than 1-minute rests on the same program. A systematic review concluded both short and long rests can build muscle, with a possible edge for longer rests in trained lifters. That is why the heavy compound lifts here use 2–3 minutes and the isolation work uses 60–90 seconds.

The 2026 ACSM position stand links hypertrophy with 10 or more sets per muscle per week, and an earlier dose-response meta-analysis showed growth keeps rising as sets increase. Chest, back, biceps, triceps and quads sit at 10–16 sets. Delts and hamstrings sit just under 10, and calves at 7. If one of these is a priority, add two sets to its session instead of adding a sixth day. Our sets per muscle group guide explains how to adjust from there.

⚠️ More sets on one day is not always better

Stacking 20+ sets for one muscle into a single session usually means the last sets are done tired and with poor technique. If you want more chest or back volume, move it to a second day (for example, two sets of push-ups or pulldowns at the end of shoulder day) instead of extending one session.

How to Progress: Double Progression

Every exercise uses a rep range. Stay at one weight until you hit the top of the range on all sets at the listed RIR, then add load and work back up. This is double progression, a simple form of progressive overload.

  1. Start conservatively. In week 1, choose loads that leave 3 reps in reserve. You are learning the exercise order and machine settings.
  2. Build reps. Example: Bench press 4 × 6–8 at 80 kg. Week 2: 8, 7, 7, 6. Week 3: 8, 8, 7, 7. Week 4: 8, 8, 8, 8.
  3. Add load. Once all sets hit 8, add the smallest jump available (2.5 kg on barbells, 1–2 kg per dumbbell) and restart near 6 reps. The ACSM progression guidelines suggest a 2–10% load increase once you can do one or two reps beyond the target.
  4. Log it. Write down weight, reps and RIR for every set.

Isolation moves progress slower than compound lifts. A lateral raise may stay at the same dumbbell for 3–4 weeks while reps climb, and that still counts as progress.

Deload Protocol

Plan a lighter week after week 7, then start a new 8-week block with slightly heavier starting weights. Deload earlier if your main lifts drop two sessions in a row, joints ache, or sleep and motivation slide.

In a survey of strength and physique athletes, deloads lasted about a week and were taken every 5–6 weeks on average, mostly by cutting sets and reps while keeping the same training days. This plan follows that pattern:

  • Keep the same five days and exercises.
  • Cut working sets roughly in half (4 sets → 2, 3 sets → 1–2).
  • Keep loads about the same or slightly lighter, and stop every set at RIR 3–4.

More detail on timing is in the deload week guide.

Warm-Up and Exercise Substitutions

Warm-up (8–10 minutes)

Do 5 minutes of easy cardio, then 2–3 ramp-up sets of the first exercise: an empty bar or light load for 8–10 reps, about 60% of your working weight for 5 reps, then about 80% for 2–3 reps. Later exercises need only one lighter feeder set.

What to Eat on a Bro Split

Two numbers matter most. Eat about 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight daily: muscle gains from protein plateaued near 1.62 g/kg in a meta-analysis of 49 trials. If you want to add size, eat slightly above maintenance; a bodybuilding nutrition review suggests a 10–20% surplus with a gain of about 0.25–0.5% of body weight per week. Bro Split vs Push Pull Legs vs Upper/Lower

All three splits build muscle when weekly sets are similar. The better choice depends on your schedule and how you recover.

A smart compromise is to run this bro split for one 8-week block, then switch to a split with twice-weekly frequency. Changing the stimulus every few months keeps training fresh without program-hopping every few weeks.

Common Bro Split Mistakes

  • Skipping legs or doing "legs lite". Thursday is the only direct quad and hamstring session. Treat it as the priority day of the week.
  • Turning arm day into a third pressing day. Extra heavy pressing on Friday cuts into Monday's recovery. Keep Friday to the listed close-grip bench sets.
  • Training everything to failure. A 2023 meta-analysis found little to no extra growth from going to failure compared with stopping just short. Use failure only on the last set of isolation moves.
  • Too many exercises. Seven or eight exercises per muscle push sessions past 90 minutes and add junk volume. Five or six well-chosen moves are enough.
  • Random weights each week. Without a log and a progression rule, a bro split turns into pump sessions with no long-term overload.

Avoid those five errors and run the plan for a full 8-week block before judging it. Take progress photos and measurements at the start and end so you judge the plan on results, not on how sore you feel.

Bro Split Workout Plan FAQ

Can I do cardio on a bro split?

Yes. Easy cardio such as walking or cycling for 20–30 minutes fits after upper-body days or on weekends. Avoid hard intervals the day before leg day so squats and Romanian deadlifts do not suffer.

Can I do a bro split at home?

Only with enough equipment. You need adjustable dumbbells, a bench and ideally a pull-up bar and bands to replace the cable and machine moves. Without them, a full-body or upper/lower plan is easier to load properly.

Can I do a bro split in 4 days?

You can combine shoulders and arms into one day, but that session runs long and side delts tend to get shortchanged. A 4-day schedule usually works better with a torso/limbs or upper/lower layout.

Should I train abs every day on a bro split?

No. Abs recover like other muscles. Two or three focused sessions a week, such as the cable crunch and hanging knee raise in this plan, are enough alongside the bracing done on squats and rows.

Can women follow a bro split?

Yes. Men and women gain muscle at similar relative rates on the same program. Women who want more glute work can add hip thrusts at the start of arm day.

What if I miss a day?

Do the missed session the next day and push the rest of the week back by one day. Avoid skipping legs; if you must drop a session, drop arms, since biceps and triceps already get indirect work.
