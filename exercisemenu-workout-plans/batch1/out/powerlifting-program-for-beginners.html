Quick answer

A good powerlifting program for beginners practices the squat, bench press and deadlift two to three times a week, uses moderate loads to build technique first, then gets heavier in planned phases. This 12-week plan runs three days a week with RPE targets and ends with a test day for all three lifts.

Key takeaways
  • Bench is trained three times a week, squat twice and deadlift once heavy plus one lighter variation.
  • Weeks 1–4 build volume at RPE 6–7, weeks 5–8 build strength at RPE 7–8, and weeks 9–11 practise heavy singles to doubles.
  • Week 12 cuts volume so you arrive at test day fresh.
  • Competition-style technique matters from day one: squat depth, a paused bench and a full deadlift lockout.
  • Rest 3–5 minutes between heavy sets of the main lifts.

Plenty of beginners are already strong enough to try powerlifting. What they lack is a plan that practises the three lifts the way they are judged, builds strength in a sensible order, and teaches them to handle heavy singles safely.

This program gives you that structure. You need about six months of general lifting experience and the ability to squat, bench and deadlift with consistent form. What Makes Powerlifting Different?

In a powerlifting meet, each lift must meet a standard. Training to those standards from week 1 means your gym numbers will count on the platform.

LiftWhat judges look forHow this plan trains it
SquatHip crease below the top of the knee, then stand to full lockoutCompetition-depth squats, plus paused squats on Day 3
Bench pressBar held motionless on the chest before pressing; shoulders and glutes stay on the bench and feet stay on the floorPaused bench on Day 2, touch-and-go only for volume
DeadliftStand fully upright with hips and knees locked, no hitchingFull lockout every rep; no bouncing between reps

Federations differ slightly in their rules, so read the rulebook of any meet you enter. For technique details, see high-bar vs low-bar squat, bench press grip width and sumo vs conventional deadlift.

How RPE Works in This Program

Loads are set by RPE (rating of perceived exertion), not fixed percentages. That lets the program adjust on days you feel strong or tired. This plan uses the reps-in-reserve RPE scale validated by Zourdos et al.:

RPEMeaningReps in reserve
10Maximal effort, no reps left0
9One more rep possible1
8Two more reps possible2
7Three more reps possible; bar moves quickly3
6Four or more reps left; warm-up feel4+

New lifters tend to underrate how many reps they have left, so be honest and conservative. The RPE calculator converts RPE and reps into an estimated one-rep max, and our RPE vs RIR guide explains the scale in more depth.

🔬 Why this frequency?

A meta-analysis on training frequency found strength gains rose with more weekly sessions, mostly because frequency allowed more total practice and volume. A network meta-analysis of 178 studies ranked higher-load, multi-set training three times a week as the most effective prescription for strength.

The Three Phases

The weekly structure stays the same for 12 weeks. What changes is sets, reps and RPE on the main lifts.

Bench (Day 1)3 × 8 @ RPE 74 × 6 @ RPE 73 × 5 @ RPE 7
Paused bench (Day 2)4 × 5 @ RPE 75 × 3 @ RPE 8Top set @ RPE 8 (3, 2, 1 reps), then 3 × 3 at −10%

Heavier work arrives late on purpose. A network meta-analysis found high loads produced the largest strength gains, and the 2026 ACSM position stand found heavier loads (80%+ of max) improved strength most. The base phase builds the muscle and technique that make those heavy weeks productive.

Rest Periods and Warm-Ups

Rest 3–5 minutes between heavy sets of the main lifts and 90 seconds to 2 minutes on accessories. A systematic review found rests over 2 minutes were needed to maximise strength gains in trained lifters.

Warm up with 5 minutes of light cardio, then ramp up gradually: empty bar × 8–10, then sets of 5, 3, 2 and 1 at roughly 40%, 60%, 75% and 85–90% of the day's top set. Use the warm-up set calculator to plan the jumps.

Week 12: Taper and Test Day

Evidence on peaking for powerlifting is limited; a review on tapering for powerlifters found few studies with enough detail to give a fixed protocol. This plan takes a conservative approach common in coaching: reduce volume, keep some heavy work, and arrive rested.

Week 12 daySession
MondaySquat 2 × 2 @ RPE 7, bench 2 × 2 @ RPE 7
WednesdayBench 3 × 3 @ RPE 6, light accessories only
SaturdayTest day: squat, bench, deadlift (3 attempts each)

Choosing your attempts

  • Opener: a weight you could triple on a bad day, roughly 90% of your estimated max.
  • Second: about 95%, a confident lift.
  • Third: 100–102% if the second moved well; repeat the second plus 2.5 kg if it was slow.

Estimate your max from week 11's top single with the 1RM calculator guide. Use spotters on squat and bench, or safety bars set just below the bottom position.

Nutrition, Recovery and Body Weight

Eat about 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of body weight daily; a meta-analysis of 49 trials found muscle gains plateaued near 1.6 g/kg. Beginners do not need to cut weight for a weight class; eat at maintenance or slightly above and let strength rise. Creatine monohydrate at 3–5 g per day is the best-supported supplement for strength training and is optional.

After the Test Day

Take an easy week, then rerun the program with your new numbers. A second 12-week cycle is usually just as productive for a beginner. Once progress across a full cycle slows, it is time for more volume and longer phases. Typical strength levels by age and body weight are in our guides to the average squat, average bench press and average deadlift.

Powerlifting Program for Beginners FAQ

How long should you lift before starting powerlifting?

About six months of consistent training is enough for most people, as long as you can squat, bench and deadlift with repeatable technique.

Do I need powerlifting gear as a beginner?

Flat-soled shoes and chalk help. A lifting belt is useful once loads get heavy but is optional. Knee sleeves and wrist wraps are personal preference.

Can I do cardio while powerlifting?

Yes. Two or three easy 20–30 minute sessions a week support health and work capacity. Avoid hard leg conditioning before Day 1 or Day 2.

Should beginners max out often?

No. Heavy singles are a skill to practise in a controlled way, which is why they appear only in weeks 9–11 at RPE 8 before a single test day.

Can women use this powerlifting program?

Yes. The lifts and progression are the same. Many women find bench progress slower in absolute kilograms, so use smaller jumps of 1–1.25 kg when available.

Do I have to compete?

No. Running the test day in your gym gives you the same benchmark. Entering a local novice meet is a good next step if you enjoy it.
