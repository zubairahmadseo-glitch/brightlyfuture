3-Day Full Body Workout for Intermediate Lifters (Heavy, Light, Medium)

Intermediate · 3 Days

Past the beginner stage but still limited to three gym days? This 3 day full body workout rotates a heavy, a light and a medium session so you can train every muscle three times a week without grinding yourself down.

The ACSM position stand associates more growth with 10+ weekly sets per muscle, and Schoenfeld et al. (2019) found strength gains were similar across low and high volumes while size kept rising with more sets. This plan sits near the 10-set mark for most muscles to keep sessions under 75 minutes. If hamstrings, side delts or calves are a priority, add 2–3 sets to the light day; see how many sets per muscle group per week for guidance on adding volume.

Progression by Day

Each day progresses differently, which is the main skill an intermediate lifter needs. Read the progressive overload guide for the wider principles.

DayRuleExample
HeavyWhen all sets hit the top of the range at RIR 2, add 2.5 kg (upper) or 5 kg (lower)Squat 4 × 6 at 100 kg → next week 4 sets at 105 kg
LightAdd reps until all sets reach 15, then add the smallest loadLeg press 12, 13, 14 reps → 15, 15, 15 → add 10 kg
MediumDouble progression within each rep rangeOHP 3 × 8 → 3 × 10 → add 2.5 kg

Progress on the light day will look slow on paper. Its job is to add volume with low joint stress, not to set records. If the heavy day stalls for two weeks, check sleep and calories before changing the program.

Deload Every 8th Week

Run weeks 1–7 as written and deload in week 8. Keep all three days, cut sets roughly in half, and stop each set at RIR 3–4. In a survey of strength and physique athletes, deloads lasted about a week, came every 5–6 weeks on average, and mostly reduced sets and reps while keeping training frequency. If you feel worn down by week 5 or 6, deload then. The deload week guide lists the warning signs.

Warm-Up

Warm up for 5–8 minutes with light cardio and a few bodyweight squats, hip hinges and band pull-aparts. Then ramp up on the first lift: bar or light load × 8, about 60% × 5, about 80% × 2–3. The warm-up guide covers this in more detail.

Why Full Body Still Works for Intermediates

Split and full-body routines produced similar strength and muscle gains when weekly volume was matched in a 2024 meta-analysis of 14 studies. Training a lift more often also helps strength: gains rose with weekly frequency in a 2018 meta-analysis, and the 2026 Pelland meta-regression found the same with diminishing returns. The heavy/light/medium rotation is a fatigue tool; the 2026 ACSM position stand found periodization models did not consistently change outcomes, so its value is practical: you grind heavy loads only once a week.

What to Eat on This Plan

Aim for about 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight daily, the point where gains plateaued in a meta-analysis of 49 trials. Spread it evenly over 3–5 meals; the ISSN protein position stand suggests 20–40 g per meal every 3–4 hours. A meal with carbs and protein 1–3 hours before training helps you start the session fuelled. For meal ideas, see the best pre-workout meals for muscle gain.

3-Day Full Body Workout FAQ

Can I do this 3 day full body workout on consecutive days?

It's not recommended. Each session trains every muscle, so back-to-back days leave you under-recovered. Keep at least 48 hours between sessions.

Is 3 days a week enough to build muscle as an intermediate?

Yes. With about 9–12 hard sets per major muscle each week and steady progression, three sessions can keep you progressing for a long time, though progress is slower than on a higher-volume 4–5 day plan.

Can I add a fourth day?

Add an optional short arms-and-delts session on Saturday (lateral raises, curls, triceps extensions, 2–3 sets each) if you have time. Keep it light enough that Monday's heavy day is not affected.

What if I miss the heavy day?

Do the heavy session at your next workout and skip the light day that week. The heavy day drives strength, so it should not be the session you drop.

Should I train abs separately?

Squats, deadlifts, split squats and overhead presses all train your trunk. If you want direct ab work, add 2–3 sets of cable crunches or hanging knee raises to the light day.
