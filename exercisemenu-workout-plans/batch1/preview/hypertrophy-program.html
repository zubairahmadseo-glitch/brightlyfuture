4-Day Hypertrophy Program: A 6-Week Muscle-Building Mesocycle

Muscle Growth Focus

This hypertrophy program has one goal: more muscle. It uses a 4-day anterior/posterior split, a 6-week block that ramps effort and sets week by week, and exercise choices that load each muscle through a full stretch.

Quick answer

This 4-day hypertrophy program alternates anterior days (quads, chest, front delts, biceps) with posterior days (back, hamstrings, glutes, triceps, calves). It runs as a 6-week mesocycle: effort rises from 3 reps in reserve to near failure, sets increase in week 3, and week 6 is a deload.

The 6-Week Mesocycle

A mesocycle is a training block that builds fatigue on purpose and then clears it. Here, you start each block comfortably and finish it working hard, then deload.

WeekSetsTarget effortGoal
1Base (as listed)RIR 3Learn loads, low soreness
2BaseRIR 2Add reps or load from week 1
3+1 set on the first two exercises each dayRIR 2Volume step
4Same as week 3RIR 1Push reps
5Same as week 3RIR 1 (compounds), 0–1 (isolation)Peak week
6Half the base setsRIR 3–4Deload and recover

Week 6 follows the consensus definition from a Delphi study of coaches and researchers: a period of reduced training stress to reduce fatigue and prepare for the next block. Athletes in a cross-sectional survey deloaded about every 5–6 weeks by cutting sets and reps, close to the rhythm used here. More on timing is in our deload week guide.

If one muscle lags after two blocks, add 2 sets for that muscle to the peak weeks. The training volume calculator helps you total your own numbers.

How to Progress Inside and Between Blocks

Inside a block: keep the same exercises for all six weeks. Each week, aim to beat last week's reps or load at the new RIR target. Because the target RIR gets lower, reps often rise even at the same weight.

Between blocks: start block two with week 1 loads about 2.5–5% above block one's week 1. Keep 70–80% of exercises; swap only ones that caused joint pain or felt like they did not train the target muscle. This keeps progressive overload measurable.

⚠️ Signs you need to deload early

Loads dropping on two sessions in a row, joint pain that lasts into the next session, or sleep and motivation falling off for a week. Take the deload, then restart the block at week 2.

Technique Rules for Every Set

  • Control the lowering. Take about 2–3 seconds on the eccentric to keep tension on the muscle and every rep consistent.
  • Full range, with a pause in the stretch. One second at the bottom of presses, curls and leg curls removes bounce.
  • Rest for performance. 2–3 minutes on compounds, 60–90 seconds on isolation work. Trained men grew more with 3-minute than 1-minute rests on compound lifts.
  • Count only hard sets. Warm-up sets do not count toward weekly volume. See warm-up sets vs working sets.

Why an Anterior/Posterior Split?

An anterior/posterior split divides the body front and back instead of upper and lower. Anterior days train quads, chest, front and side delts, and biceps. Posterior days train back, hamstrings, glutes, rear delts, triceps and calves.

  • Balanced session length. Each day mixes one big lower-body lift with upper-body work, so no day is a 75-minute leg session.
  • Less overlap. Biceps are trained on chest days and triceps on back days, so arms are fresh for their direct work.
  • Two exposures per muscle. Each anterior and posterior session runs twice a week.

Why This Program Works for Hypertrophy

Four variables do most of the work. The 2026 ACSM position stand, which pooled 137 systematic reviews, found muscle growth was enhanced by higher weekly volume (10 or more sets per muscle) and eccentric overload, while many other details had inconsistent effects.

Two more details matter: rest long enough to keep rep quality high, and train through a full range of motion with emphasis on the stretched position.

🔬 Long muscle lengths

In a within-person trial, seated leg curls (hamstrings stretched at the hip) built about 14% more hamstring volume versus 9% for lying curls. A 2025 study in trained lifters found that partial reps in the stretched position produced similar growth to full reps on upper-body exercises. This plan uses full range of motion and picks exercises that stretch the target muscle under load.

Nutrition for Hypertrophy

Eat about 1.6–2.2 g of protein per kg of body weight daily; gains from protein plateaued around 1.62 g/kg in a meta-analysis of 49 trials. A small calorie surplus can speed growth, though the ideal size of that surplus is not known; a weight gain of about 0.25–0.5% of body weight per week is a sensible target. Hypertrophy Program FAQ

Can I swap exercises in this program?

Yes. Replace an exercise with one that trains the same muscle through a similar range of motion, such as a hack squat for a back squat or a lat pulldown for pull-ups, and keep the swap for the whole 6-week block.

Should I do cardio on a hypertrophy program?

Yes, in moderate amounts. Two or three easy 20–30 minute sessions a week support heart health. Keep hard intervals away from the day before an anterior day, since they can affect quad performance.

Can I run this hypertrophy program while cutting?

Yes, but keep sets at the week 1–2 base level for the whole block, because recovery is often lower in a calorie deficit. Keep loads as heavy as possible to hold muscle.

How many blocks should I run before changing programs?

Two to three 6-week blocks is typical. Change the program when progress stalls across a full block despite good sleep and nutrition, not because of boredom.

Is this hypertrophy program suitable for women?

Yes. Women and men grow at similar relative rates on the same program. Women who want more glute work can swap one chest set for an extra set of hip thrusts or split squats.

Can I run this program on 3 days a week?

Yes, by rotating the four sessions: Anterior A, Posterior A and Anterior B one week, then Posterior B, Anterior A and Posterior A the next. Each muscle is then trained about 1.5 times a week, so expect slightly slower progress.
