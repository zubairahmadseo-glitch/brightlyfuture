Arm Workout: Biceps and Triceps Plan for Gym or Home

Body Part Plan · Arms

Two arm workouts a week that train every part of your biceps and triceps: a heavy curl, an overhead triceps move, a press and a hammer curl for the forearms. Choose the gym, dumbbell or band version and add a rep or a little weight each week.

Quick answer

A good arm workout pairs 2–3 biceps curls with 2–3 triceps exercises, including one overhead extension. Train arms twice a week for about 12–16 sets per muscle, use 8–15 reps, stop 1–2 reps short of failure and add reps or weight every week.

Keep the two arm sessions 2–3 days apart. You can run them as stand-alone arm days or add them to the end of an upper-body, push or pull day. If you already press and row heavy the day before, start with the dumbbell version so your elbows aren't overloaded.

The Arm Workouts

Rest 60–90 seconds between sets, or pair one biceps and one triceps exercise as a superset to save time. RIR (reps in reserve) 1 means you finish the set with one clean rep left.

🔬 What the research shows

Overhead work matters for the triceps: in a 12-week study, cable extensions done with the arm overhead grew the whole triceps about 1.4 times more than the same exercise with the arm at the side. For the biceps, a 2021 study found that biceps curls built about twice as much elbow-flexor muscle as dumbbell rows in beginners, so rows alone won't max out your biceps.

The incline curl and the overhead extension both load the muscle while it is stretched, which is why each workout includes one of them.

How Many Sets for Arms per Week?

This plan gives each muscle about 12–16 direct sets a week. Rows and pull-downs also train your biceps, and presses train your triceps, so count each pulling or pressing set as about half a set for your arms, as the 2026 dose-response meta-analysis did. That means if you already do 10 sets of rows a week, your biceps get roughly 5 extra sets from them.

Training a muscle twice a week builds more muscle than once a week in a meta-analysis of training frequency, and the 2026 ACSM position stand links 10 or more weekly sets per muscle with more growth. If your elbows get sore, drop one exercise per workout before you drop a session. Our guide to sets per muscle group shows how to adjust volume.

How to Progress Your Arm Workout

Arm exercises use small weights, so progress with reps first. Stay at one weight until you hit the top of the rep range on every set, then move up by the smallest step available: 1–2 kg per dumbbell or one pin on a cable stack. A 2 kg jump on a 10 kg curl is a 20% increase, so expect reps to drop and build back up. That is progressive overload for small muscles.

Every 6–8 weeks, swap one exercise per muscle (for example, preacher curl to cable curl) and take a lighter week with half the sets.

Arm Workout Mistakes

  • Swinging the weight. If your hips or shoulders move, the load is too heavy. Keep your upper arms still and lower each rep in about two seconds.
  • Short reps. Straighten your arm fully at the bottom of curls and bend fully on extensions; the stretched part of the rep is where much of the growth comes from.
  • Only doing biceps. Skipping triceps leaves the bigger part of your arm untrained.
  • Training arms on the day before a heavy press or pull day. Tired arms limit your bench press and rows. Put arm work after those sessions, not before them.

Arm Workout FAQ

Can I do an arm workout every day?

It's not a good idea. Your biceps and triceps also work during every pull and press, so daily arm sessions leave them with no time to recover. Two or three arm sessions a week is enough to grow.

Will arm exercises get rid of flabby arms?

No exercise burns fat from one spot. In a 6-week study on ab training, targeted exercise didn't reduce fat in the trained area. Arm training builds the muscle underneath, and overall fat loss comes from your diet and total activity.

Are pull-ups and push-ups enough for arms?

They train your arms, but less than direct work does. If arm size is a goal, add at least 2–3 sets of curls and triceps extensions twice a week.

How long until my arms get bigger?

Training studies usually measure arm growth after 8–12 weeks of twice-weekly training. Arm size changes slowly, so track your curl and extension weights to see progress sooner.

Should I train biceps or triceps first?

Start with whichever is weaker. Pairing a biceps and a triceps exercise as a superset also works and shortens the session.
