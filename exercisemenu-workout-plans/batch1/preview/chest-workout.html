Chest Workout: Best Chest Exercises and a 4-Week Plan

Body Part Plan · Chest

Two chest workouts a week, built around presses at two bench angles, a fly and dips or push-ups. Pick the gym, dumbbell or no-equipment version and follow the 4-week progression.

Put the two chest workouts 2–3 days apart, such as Monday and Thursday, and train your other muscles on the remaining days. Workout A leads with the flat bench press; Workout B leads with an incline press, so across the week you train every part of the chest with heavy and moderate loads.

Best Chest Exercises by Area

Your pectoralis major has an upper (clavicular) part and a larger middle-to-lower (sternal) part, and the bench angle changes which part works hardest.

ExerciseMain areaWhy it's in the plan
Flat barbell or dumbbell bench pressMiddle and lower chestHeaviest chest lift and the easiest to track
Incline press (about 30°)Upper chest30° gave the highest upper-chest activity in an EMG study
Dips (leaning forward)Lower chest, tricepsLoads the chest through a deep stretch
Cable or dumbbell flyWhole chestTrains the chest without the triceps limiting the set
Push-upWhole chest, tricepsBuilds muscle like a light bench press when the load is similar
🔬 Flat vs incline

In a study of five bench angles, upper-chest activity peaked at 30°, while angles above 45° shifted the work to the front delts. In an 8-week training study, the incline group grew more upper-chest muscle than the flat-bench group, with similar strength gains. That's why Workout B uses a 30° incline instead of a steep one.

There is no separate "inner chest" muscle to isolate; the fibres run across the whole chest, so a full range of motion on presses and flyes trains the inner area too. For more cable options, see our cable chest exercises.

4-Week Progression

WeekSetsEffortWhat to do
1As listedRIR 3Learn loads and settings
2As listedRIR 2Beat week 1 by a rep or a little weight
3+1 set on the first exerciseRIR 1–2Push the main press
4Same as week 3RIR 1Hardest week; then repeat with heavier starting loads

Within each week, use double progression: once every set reaches the top of its rep range, add the smallest jump available (2.5 kg on a barbell, 1–2 kg per dumbbell) and build the reps back up. That is the core of progressive overload. After two 4-week blocks, take a lighter week with half the sets.

How Many Chest Sets per Week?

The two gym workouts give you about 20 hard chest sets a week, and the dumbbell and home versions about 24, because you do the same workout twice. The 2026 ACSM position stand links more muscle growth with 10 or more sets per muscle per week, and a dose-response meta-analysis found growth kept rising as sets increased. If you are new to lifting or your shoulders feel beaten up, use 2 sets per exercise (about 14 sets a week) and add sets as you recover well. Our guide to sets per muscle group per week explains how to adjust.

How to Fit These Chest Workouts Into Your Split

  • Push pull legs: use Workout A on your first push day and Workout B on the second. See our push pull legs workout plan.
  • Upper/lower: do the first two exercises of each workout on your upper days.
  • Bro split: run Workout A on chest day and add 4–6 sets from Workout B on shoulder or arm day so the chest gets a second weekly session.

Whatever split you run, keep 2–3 days between the two chest sessions.

Chest Workout Mistakes

  • Bouncing the bar. Touch the chest under control; the bounce takes work away from the muscle you are training. Our bench press guide covers the setup.
  • Inclines that are too steep. Above 45° the front delts take over. Keep the bench at about 30°.
  • Half reps on flyes and presses. Lower until you feel a stretch across the chest, then press.
  • Grinding every set to failure. Training to failure built no more muscle than stopping short in a 2023 meta-analysis, and it adds fatigue, so save failure for the last set of flyes or push-ups.

Chest Workout FAQ

Do I need the bench press to build my chest?

No. Dumbbell presses, machine presses and loaded push-ups all build the chest. The barbell bench press is simply the easiest to load heavy and track over time.

How long until I see chest growth?

Training studies measure chest growth after about 8 weeks. Strength usually rises sooner, and visible change depends on your starting point and diet.

Should I train chest and triceps together?

Yes, it works well. Your triceps already help on every press, so finishing a chest workout with 2–3 sets of triceps work is time-efficient.

Can women follow this chest workout?

Yes. The exercises and sets are the same; many women start with dumbbells or the no-equipment version and progress from there.

Why do my shoulders take over on presses?

Usually the bench is too steep or the shoulder blades aren't pulled back. Keep inclines around 30°, set your shoulder blades back and down, and lower the bar to mid-chest.
