Resistance Band Workout: 3-Day Full Body Plan for Home

Home Workout Plan · Bands

Three full-body band workouts a week that train your legs, chest, back, shoulders, arms and core with nothing but a few resistance bands and a door anchor. Follow Workout A and B, or start with the 25-minute beginner version, and make the bands harder every week.

The Resistance Band Workouts

Choose a band that lets you finish the listed reps with 1–2 clean reps left (RIR 1–2). Rest 60–90 seconds between sets, and warm up with 5 minutes of easy movement plus a light set of the first two exercises. New to training? Start with the beginner tab for 3–4 weeks, then move to Workouts A and B.

Alternate Workout A and B on three non-consecutive days: A–B–A one week, B–A–B the next. Both workouts train your whole body, so each major muscle is worked three times a week with a rest day in between. Add the mini-band finisher to the end of any session if your hips and glutes need extra work.

Which Resistance Bands You Need

Band typeWhat it looks likeUse it for
Long loop band (power band)A long closed loop, usually about 104 cm (41 in)Squats, Romanian deadlifts, presses, rows, push-ups
Tube band with handlesTube with two handles, clips onto a door anchorChest press, pulldowns, face pulls, curls
Mini loop bandSmall loop worn around the thighs or anklesGlute bridges, side steps, hip abduction

Get at least three resistance levels (light, medium, heavy). Colours mean different resistances from brand to brand, so pick by feel: the right band is the one that makes the last reps of the set hard. Your legs and back will need heavier bands than your shoulders and arms.

🔬 Are bands as good as weights?

For strength, the research says yes. A meta-analysis of eight studies found elastic bands and tubes built upper- and lower-body strength as well as machines and dumbbells. In a 5-week study, trained lifters who did band push-ups gained as much bench press strength as those who did the bench press at a similar effort.

How to Progress With Resistance Bands

Bands don't come in 2.5 kg steps, so progress in this order. Move to the next step once you reach the top of the rep range on every set:

StepWhat to changeExample
1Add repsSquat: 3 × 12 → 3 × 15
2Slow the lowering to 3 seconds and pause where the band is tightestRow: 1-second squeeze at the top
3Add tension: step farther from the anchor or shorten the bandTake one step back on chest presses
4Move to a heavier band, or use two bands togetherMedium + light band on squats

A band gets harder as it stretches, so the top of each rep is the hardest part and the bottom is the easiest. Start every rep with a little tension on the band so the bottom still works. Our guide to progressive overload without adding weight has more options.

How Many Sets and Reps With Resistance Bands?

Use 10–20 reps per set and stop 1–2 reps short of failure. Higher reps are fine with bands: light loads built similar muscle to heavy loads in a network meta-analysis when sets were taken close to failure, while heavier loads built a little more maximal strength. Alternating A and B gives your quads, chest and back about 9 hard sets a week and your glutes more. The 2026 ACSM position stand links 10 or more weekly sets per muscle with more growth, so once you recover well, add one set to the first two exercises of each workout. Our guide to sets per muscle group shows how to adjust.

Resistance Band Mistakes and Safety

  • Using a worn band. Check every band for nicks, cracks or thin spots before each session, and replace it if you find one. A band that snaps can hit your face.
  • Anchoring on the wrong side of the door. Fit the door anchor on the hinge side and stand on the side the door opens away from, so pulling presses the door shut. Lock it if you can.
  • Letting the band snap back. Lower every rep under control; the slow return trains your muscles too.
  • Staying on the same band for months. If you can do more than 20 easy reps, the band is too light. Use the progression steps above.

Resistance Band Workout FAQ

Can you build muscle with resistance bands?

Yes, as long as your sets are hard. Muscle grows from sets taken close to failure, and bands let you do that at home. Move to heavier bands as you get stronger and eat enough protein; beginners usually see the fastest progress.

Are resistance bands better than dumbbells?

Neither is better for building strength; the research shows similar gains. Dumbbells are easier to load in small steps, while bands are cheaper, lighter and easier to store, which makes them a good choice for home and travel.

Do resistance bands lose resistance over time?

Yes. Latex bands slowly wear with use, heat and sunlight. Store them out of direct sun, keep them away from sharp edges, and replace any band that has cracks, nicks or no longer snaps back fully.

Can I combine resistance bands with dumbbells?

Yes. A simple setup is dumbbells for squats, lunges and Romanian deadlifts, and bands for rows, pulldowns, face pulls and pull-aparts, which are hard to do with dumbbells alone.

What if I don't have a door anchor?

Loop the band around a sturdy post or railing, or stand on it. Squats, Romanian deadlifts, rows, presses, curls and push-ups all work with the band under your feet or across your back.
