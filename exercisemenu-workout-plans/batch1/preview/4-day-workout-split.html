Quick answer

A good 4-day workout split trains each muscle twice a week, which keeps sessions shorter and helps strength. This plan alternates torso days (chest, back, shoulders) with limbs days (legs, biceps, triceps) on Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday, using one heavier and one higher-rep version of each day.

4-Day Workout Split Schedule

The week runs on two on, one off, two on, two off. Each muscle gets one heavy and one volume session with at least 72 hours between them.

🔬 Why mix heavy and moderate loads?

A network meta-analysis of 28 studies found muscle growth was similar across light, moderate and heavy loads, but strength gains were greater with heavier loads. A 2023 Bayesian network meta-analysis ranked higher-load, multi-set training two to three times a week as the top prescriptions for strength and size. A/B rotation gives you both: heavy work for strength, moderate-rep volume for size.

Progression Rules

A days (heavy): when every set reaches the top of the range at RIR 2 (e.g., 4 × 7 on bench), add 2.5 kg to upper-body lifts or 5 kg to squats. If you miss the bottom of the range two sessions in a row, drop the load 5–10% and build back up.

B days (volume): add reps until every set reaches the top of the range, then add the smallest available load. The ACSM progression model recommends 2–10% load jumps once you exceed the target reps by one or two. Log every session; the workout tracking guide shows a simple format, and progressive overload explains the principle.

Deload and Long-Term Plan

Train weeks 1–7 as written, then deload in week 8. Keep the same four days and exercises, cut sets by about half, and stop every set at RIR 3–4. A Delphi consensus of coaches and researchers defines a deload as a planned period of reduced training stress to manage fatigue and prepare for the next block.

After the deload, start block two with loads about 2.5–5% heavier than block one's week 1. Swap 1–2 accessory exercises per day if they feel stale, but keep the main lifts so progress stays measurable. Our deload week guide covers signs you need one earlier.

Who This 4-Day Split Is For

  • 6+ months of consistent training with good form on the bench press, squat, row and hinge.
  • Four training days (ideally Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday) and 60–75 minutes per session.
  • You want strength and size together, with arms trained directly twice a week.

New to lifting? Start with a full-body plan for 3–6 months; our upper/lower vs full body guide compares the two.

What to Eat on This Plan

Muscle gains from protein plateaued at intakes around 1.6 g/kg/day in a meta-analysis of 49 trials, and a review of off-season bodybuilding nutrition recommends a slow gain of 0.25–0.5% of body weight per week to limit fat gain. If you want to lose fat while running this plan, keep protein high and keep the training loads; the program itself does not need to change. Work out your number with the protein calculator.

Mistakes That Stall a 4-Day Split

  • Torso A and Torso B on back-to-back days. The second session is usually weaker.
  • Short rests on heavy lifts. Trained lifters gained more strength with rests longer than 2 minutes; keep 2–3 minutes on A-day compounds.
  • Changing exercises every week. Keep the main lifts for the full 8-week block so you can see progress.

4-Day Workout Split FAQ

Can I add a fifth day for arms or weak points?

Yes, if you recover well. Keep it short (30–40 minutes) and light, for example lateral raises, curls and triceps work for 2–3 sets each, and place it on Saturday so it does not crowd Torso A.

Can I do cardio on the off days?

Yes. Easy cardio such as walking or cycling for 20–40 minutes on Wednesday or the weekend supports heart health without hurting your lifting. Keep hard intervals away from the day before Limbs A so your squats do not suffer.

Can beginners use a 4-day workout split?

Beginners can, but most progress faster on three full-body sessions a week while learning the lifts. After 3–6 months of steady progress, a 4-day split adds the extra volume you will start to need.

Is torso/limbs better than upper/lower?

Neither is better for growth when weekly sets are equal. Torso/limbs gives arms a dedicated slot twice a week, while upper/lower is simpler to set up and keeps all leg work on dedicated lower-body days.

Can I do this 4-day split with only dumbbells?

Mostly. Swap barbell lifts for dumbbell versions, pulldowns for pull-ups or band-assisted pull-ups, and cable moves for dumbbell flyes and raises. Leg training is the hardest part to load at home, so use split squats and single-leg work.

Can I train four days in a row?

You can, but performance on day four may drop. If your schedule forces it, run Torso A, Limbs A, Torso B, Limbs B and accept slightly lower loads on the last day.
