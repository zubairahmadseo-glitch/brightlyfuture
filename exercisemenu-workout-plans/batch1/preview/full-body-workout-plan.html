Quick answer

This beginner gym workout plan has three full-body workouts: Day A (squat focus), Day B (bench press focus) and Day C (deadlift focus), done on non-consecutive days such as Monday, Wednesday and Friday. Each takes 45–55 minutes, and you add weight once you reach the top of each rep range.

Any three days work as long as there is a rest day between sessions: Monday/Wednesday/Friday or Tuesday/Thursday/Saturday are the most common. Each day starts with a different main lift, so you practise the squat, bench press and deadlift every week while every muscle is trained three times. The ACSM progression guidelines recommend 2–3 sessions a week and sets of 8–12 reps for people new to lifting, which matches this plan.

⚠️ New to deadlifts?

For the first two weeks, use a trap bar deadlift or a dumbbell Romanian deadlift to learn the hip hinge, then move to the barbell. Read how to deadlift for beginners before your first heavy session.

8-Week Progression

WeeksPhaseWhat to do
1–2LearnLight weights, bottom of each rep range, focus on technique. Film your squat, bench press and deadlift.
3–4LoadAdd weight until the last 2–3 reps of each set feel hard (RIR 2–3).
5–7BuildDouble progression: when all sets reach the top of the range, add weight and drop back to the bottom. Stop sets at RIR 1–2.
8TestWork up to a hard set of 5 on squat, bench press and deadlift. Compare with week 1, take 2–3 days off, then restart with your new weights.

Add the smallest jump your gym allows: 1–2.5 kg on upper-body lifts and 2.5–5 kg on lower-body lifts. Example: squats call for 3 × 8–10; once you hit 10, 10, 10, add 2.5–5 kg and start again at 8. More examples are in progressive overload for beginners.

🔬 How fast beginners change

Strength rises quickly at first, partly because your nervous system gets better at each lift. Muscle grows early too: in a training study, increases in thigh muscle size were measurable after about 3 weeks of training three times a week.

Form Cues for the Three Main Lifts

Back squat

  • Bar on your upper back, feet about shoulder-width, toes slightly out.
  • Brace your core, then bend hips and knees together.
  • Go down until your hip crease reaches knee level or lower; drive up through the whole foot.

Bench press

  • Pull your shoulder blades back and down before unracking.
  • Lower the bar to mid-chest with forearms roughly vertical.
  • Keep feet flat and press the bar up and slightly back over the shoulders. Full guide: how to bench press.

Deadlift

  • Bar over mid-foot, hands just outside the legs.
  • Chest up, take the slack out of the bar before pulling.
  • Push the floor away, keep the bar close, and finish by standing tall without leaning back.

If your form breaks down on any rep, the weight is too heavy; drop it 10–20% and build back up.

Warm-Up (5 Minutes + Ramp-Up Sets)

Do 3 minutes of easy cardio, then arm circles, leg swings and 10–15 bodyweight squats. Before your first barbell lift, do an empty-bar set of 10 and a set of 5 at about 50–60% of your working weight. The warm-up set calculator works out the numbers.

What to Eat as a Beginner

Aim for about 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight a day; gains in muscle plateaued around that intake in a meta-analysis of 49 trials. For a 75 kg person that is about 120 g, spread over 3–4 meals. If your goal is muscle, eat at maintenance or slightly above; if you want to lose fat, a moderate deficit still lets most beginners get stronger. Use the protein calculator to set your target.

Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

  • Adding weight too fast. Increase only when every set reaches the top of the rep range with clean form.
  • Going to failure every set. Keep 1–3 reps in reserve during these 8 weeks.
  • Not tracking. Write down every set; see the workout tracking guide.
  • Program hopping. Finish all 8 weeks before changing anything.

Beginner Gym Workout Plan FAQ

What weight should I start with?

Start with the empty barbell (20 kg men's bar, 15 kg women's bar) and light dumbbells. If the bar is too heavy for the bench press, use dumbbells until it isn't.

Can I do cardio on rest days?

Yes. Two or three 20–30 minute sessions of walking, easy cycling or swimming are a good start. Keep hard sprints or long runs away from the day before Day A.

What if my gym has no barbell or squat rack?

Use goblet squats for back squats, the dumbbell bench press for the barbell bench press and the dumbbell Romanian deadlift for the deadlift. The progression rules stay the same.

Can I follow this plan while losing weight?

Yes. Eat in a moderate deficit, keep protein at 1.6–2.0 g/kg and follow the plan as written. Large calorie deficits make it harder to keep muscle and train well.

When will I see results?

Strength often improves within 2–3 weeks. Visible muscle changes usually take 6–8 weeks or more, and bigger changes take several months of steady training.

What should I do after 8 weeks?

Repeat the 8 weeks with heavier starting weights, or move to a 4-day upper/lower split if you can add a day. Many beginners stay on full body for 3–6 months.
