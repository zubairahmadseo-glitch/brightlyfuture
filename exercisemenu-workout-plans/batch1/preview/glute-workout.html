Glute Workout: 2-Day Plan to Build Your Glutes

Body Part Plan · Glutes

Two glute workouts a week built on hip thrusts and deep squats, which grew glute muscle in MRI studies, plus Romanian deadlifts, lunges and abduction work for the sides of your hips. Follow the gym or home version and add load every week.

Quick answer

The best glute workout combines a hip thrust, a deep squat or lunge, a hip hinge such as the Romanian deadlift, and a hip abduction exercise. Train glutes twice a week with about 12 hard sets per session, and add weight or reps every week.

🔬 Hip thrust or squat?

Both. In a 9-week MRI study, hip thrusts and back squats grew the glutes by a similar amount, while squats also grew the quads and inner thighs more. Hip thrusts showed more glute activity on EMG, but that didn't predict who grew more. Depth matters for squats: full squats grew the glutes about three times more than half squats in a 10-week study.

In the hip thrust study, the gluteus medius and minimus barely grew from either exercise, which is why each workout ends with an abduction exercise.

How to Progress Your Glute Workout

Use double progression: keep the same weight until every set reaches the top of its rep range, then add 2.5–5 kg on the hip thrust and squat or move up one dumbbell size on lunges. On abduction machines, add one plate. Write your numbers down; small weekly increases are what keep your glutes growing. Our guide to progressive overload explains the method in detail.

After 6–8 weeks, take a lighter week with half the sets, then start again a little heavier.

Can You Build Glutes Without Weights?

Yes, up to a point. Light loads built similar muscle to heavy loads in a meta-analysis when sets were taken close to failure. At home, that means slowing your reps, pausing at the bottom and doing single-leg versions. Once bodyweight sets pass 25–30 reps, a pair of dumbbells or a loop band will make each set count again.

Glute Workout Mistakes

  • Arching your back on hip thrusts. Tuck your chin and ribs and finish the rep with your glutes, so your hips stop where your body forms a straight line.
  • Half squats. Squat as deep as you can with a neutral back; shallow reps grow the glutes far less.
  • Too many light activation moves. A few band exercises warm you up, but they don't replace hard sets of heavy exercises.
  • Changing the plan every week. Repeat the same exercises long enough to add weight to them.

Glute Workout FAQ

How long until I see results from glute workouts?

Studies such as the hip thrust and squat trials above measured glute growth after 9–10 weeks. Strength rises sooner, often within the first month, and visible change depends on your starting point.

Is it OK to train glutes every day?

Not with hard sessions. Glutes need time to recover like other muscles, and they also work in squats, deadlifts and lunges on your leg days. Two hard sessions a week, with 2–3 days between them, is enough.

Can I target my lower glutes or upper glutes?

Not in isolation. Hip thrusts, squats and Romanian deadlifts train the whole gluteus maximus, and abduction exercises add work for the upper side of the hips through the gluteus medius.

Do I need to eat more to grow my glutes?

You need enough protein: about 1.6 g per kg of body weight a day suits most lifters in a large meta-analysis, and our protein calculator works out your number. A large calorie deficit makes building muscle harder, so eat at maintenance or slightly above it while glute growth is your goal.

Are glute kickbacks worth doing?

They're fine as an extra, but they are hard to load heavily. Spend most of your sets on hip thrusts, squats, lunges and hinges, then add kickbacks if you have time.
