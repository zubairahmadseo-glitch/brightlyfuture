4-Day Upper Lower Split Workout Plan (With Progression)

4 Days · 2× Frequency

Upper body twice and lower body twice a week, with three rest days. Compound lifts come first, isolation work after, and a simple progression rule tells you when to add weight.

Quick answer

This upper lower split trains Upper A, Lower A, Upper B and Lower B each week, for example Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday. Upper A leads with the bench press, Upper B with the barbell row; Lower A is squat-focused and Lower B is hip-hinge-focused. Add weight once every set reaches the top of its rep range.

The one rule: keep at least one rest day between the two upper sessions and between the two lower sessions. Option A is the most common; option B spreads sessions out if you recover slowly. Training each muscle twice a week grew more muscle than once a week in a 2016 meta-analysis.

The Upper Lower Workouts

Pick a day. Compound lifts use RPE 7–8 (finish with 2–3 reps left); isolation lifts use RPE 8–9 (1–2 reps left). Read the RPE vs RIR guide if the scale is new to you.

How to Progress Each Week

Use double progression: add reps each week within the range, then add weight once all sets reach the top. Example for a lift programmed at 4 × 6–8:

WeekWeightSets × repsWhat happens
180 kg4 × 6Start at the bottom of the range
280 kg4 × 7Add 1 rep per set
380 kg4 × 8Top of the range: increase next week
482.5 kg4 × 6Add 2.5 kg, back to the bottom

Isolation exercises follow the same system with 1–2 kg jumps. Going to failure on compound lifts adds fatigue with little extra growth; a 2023 meta-analysis found only a trivial difference between training to failure and stopping short. Save near-failure sets for the last set of isolation work. More on the method: what is progressive overload.

Deloads and Rest Days

When your main lifts stall for two sessions in a row at the same weight and reps, take a deload week: same exercises and weights, about half the sets (4 sets → 2). Then resume the progression.

On rest days, a 20–30 minute walk or a short mobility session is enough. Take an extra day off if joint soreness doesn't fade during your warm-up or your sleep has been poor for several nights. The rest day vs active recovery guide explains the options.

Common Mistakes

  • Skipping Lower B. It carries most of the week's hamstring work. If you must miss it, move it to the next free day.
  • Adding lots of extra exercises. If you want more volume, add one set to an existing exercise instead.
  • Not logging workouts. Double progression only works when you know last session's exact weights and reps.

Upper Lower Split FAQ

Can beginners follow this upper lower split?

Yes, after about 2–3 months of regular training and once you can squat, bench and row with confidence. Complete beginners usually do better with a 3-day full-body plan first.

What if I can only train three days this week?

Run Upper A, Lower A and Upper B, then start next week with Lower B. Every workout still gets done over two weeks. If three days is your normal schedule, a full-body plan suits you better.

Can I do cardio with this plan?

Yes. Easy cardio on rest days or after upper sessions works well. Keep hard intervals away from the day before a lower session.

When should I switch to a different program?

Run it for at least 12 weeks. Switch when progress stalls for 3+ weeks despite a deload, when your goals change, or when you can train 5–6 days a week.
