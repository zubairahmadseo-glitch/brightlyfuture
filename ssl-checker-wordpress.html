What Is an SSL Certificate Checker?

An SSL certificate checker is a tool that verifies the security status of a website's SSL/TLS certificate. SSL (Secure Sockets Layer) and its successor TLS (Transport Layer Security) are cryptographic protocols that encrypt data between a user's browser and a web server. When a site has a valid SSL certificate, the browser displays a padlock icon and uses HTTPS instead of HTTP.

Checking SSL certificates matters because expired, misconfigured, or missing certificates create security vulnerabilities and erode visitor trust. Browsers like Chrome and Firefox display prominent "Not Secure" warnings for sites without valid SSL, which drives visitors away and hurts search engine rankings.

This free SSL certificate checker uses Certificate Transparency logs and DNS resolution to verify your domain's certificate status, identify the issuing authority, check expiry dates, and assign a security grade from A (excellent) to F (failing). It gives you a quick snapshot of your site's SSL health without needing any technical expertise.

Understanding SSL/TLS Certificates

SSL certificates come in three validation levels, each providing a different degree of identity verification and trust.

DV - Domain Validation

Verifies only that you own or control the domain. The Certificate Authority confirms domain ownership through email, DNS record, or file upload. This is the fastest and cheapest option, often free through providers like Let's Encrypt. Best for personal websites, blogs, and small projects.

OV - Organization Validation

Verifies domain ownership plus the legal identity of your organization. The CA checks business registration documents and confirms the organization exists. Takes 1 to 3 business days to issue. Best for business websites, corporate portals, and applications that handle user data.

EV - Extended Validation

The highest level of validation, requiring thorough vetting of your organization's legal, physical, and operational existence. The CA verifies government records, physical address, and authorized contacts. Takes up to 2 weeks. Best for e-commerce sites, banks, and high-trust applications.

Why SSL Matters for SEO

Google confirmed HTTPS as a ranking signal back in 2014, and its importance has only grown since then. Websites with valid SSL certificates receive a small but meaningful boost in search engine rankings compared to HTTP-only sites. While SSL alone will not push a site to position one, it is one of the many signals that Google considers when determining where to rank your pages.

Beyond rankings, SSL directly affects user behavior metrics that influence SEO. When Chrome displays a "Not Secure" warning on an HTTP page, visitors are more likely to bounce immediately. High bounce rates and low dwell time send negative signals to search engines, potentially dragging your rankings down over time.

Mixed content is another common SSL-related SEO issue. If your site loads over HTTPS but includes images, scripts, or stylesheets from HTTP sources, browsers may block that content or display warnings. This can break page functionality and harm Core Web Vitals scores, which are also ranking factors. Always audit your site for mixed content after installing an SSL certificate to ensure everything loads securely.

Common SSL Errors and How to Fix Them

ERR_CERT_DATE_INVALID

Your SSL certificate has expired. Renew it through your Certificate Authority or hosting provider. If you use Let's Encrypt, check that your auto-renewal cron job or certbot timer is working correctly.

ERR_CERT_COMMON_NAME_INVALID

The domain in your browser does not match the domain on the certificate. This happens when you access a site via a subdomain or alias not covered by the cert. Get a certificate that includes all your domain variations, or use a wildcard certificate.

ERR_CERT_AUTHORITY_INVALID

The certificate was issued by an untrusted Certificate Authority, or it is self-signed. Replace it with a certificate from a trusted public CA. Self-signed certificates should only be used in development environments, never in production.

NET_ERR_CERT_REVOKED

The Certificate Authority has revoked this certificate, usually due to a security compromise or key leak. You need to generate a new private key and request a completely new certificate from your CA.

Mixed Content Warnings

Your HTTPS page loads some resources (images, scripts, CSS) over plain HTTP. Update all resource URLs to use HTTPS, or use protocol-relative URLs. Check your database and theme files for hardcoded HTTP links.

ERR_SSL_PROTOCOL_ERROR

The server's SSL/TLS configuration is incorrect or uses outdated protocols. Ensure your server supports TLS 1.2 or TLS 1.3 and has disabled older protocols like SSL 3.0 and TLS 1.0. Check your web server configuration file for proper cipher suite settings.

Do's and Don'ts of SSL Management

Do's

  • Renew your SSL certificate well before it expires, ideally 30 days in advance
  • Enable auto-renewal with your CA or hosting provider to avoid lapses
  • Set up 301 redirects from HTTP to HTTPS for all pages
  • Regularly audit your site for mixed content issues after enabling SSL
  • Monitor your certificate health monthly using tools like this SSL checker

Don'ts

  • Ignore browser security warnings about certificate problems
  • Use self-signed certificates on production or public-facing websites
  • Forget to update your SSL certificate after a domain name change
  • Let certificates expire, causing downtime and lost visitor trust
  • Skip regular certificate monitoring, assuming everything is always fine
