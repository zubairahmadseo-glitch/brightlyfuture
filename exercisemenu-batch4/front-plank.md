## Quick Answer (AEO)

The front plank is a bodyweight hold for the abs. Rest on your forearms with your elbows under your shoulders, straighten your legs, then lift your body into one straight line from head to heels and hold. Keep breathing. Start with 2 to 3 holds of 20 to 45 seconds.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Rectus Abdominis — holds the ribcage and pelvis together so the lower back does not sag.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
External and Internal Obliques — stop the torso twisting or tipping.
Transversus Abdominis — the deep abdominal wall that braces the trunk.
Erector Spinae — work with the abs to hold the spine still.
Gluteus Maximus and Quadriceps — keep the hips and knees straight.
Serratus Anterior — holds your shoulder blades flat against your ribcage.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set your forearms | Lie face down and place your elbows directly under your shoulders, close to your sides, with your palms down and hands facing forward. |
| 2 | Set your legs | Straighten your legs behind you and pull your toes toward your shins so the balls of your feet are on the floor. |
| 3 | Brace before you lift | Tighten your abs as if you were about to be poked in the stomach. Squeeze your glutes. |
| 4 | Lift into the plank | Lift your whole body off the floor in one piece, so you form a straight line from head to heels. Keep your shoulders stacked over your elbows and do not shrug. |
| 5 | Hold and breathe | Hold the position, breathing normally. Stop the set when your hips start to drop or lift rather than when a timer runs out. |
| 6 | Lower in one piece | Keep your trunk stiff and your knees straight as you lower back down to the floor. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Hips Sagging Toward the Floor:** When the abs tire, the hips drop and the lower back takes the strain. End the set at that point rather than holding on.
2. **Hips Hiked Up:** ACE's plank instructions say not to let the hips hike up toward the ceiling. A raised backside makes the hold easier and takes the work off the abs.
3. **Shoulders Shrugged:** Letting your shoulders creep toward your ears shifts the effort to the traps. Keep them pulled down.
4. **Elbows Too Far Forward:** Elbows out in front of the shoulders change the angle and load the shoulder joint. Stack them directly underneath.
5. **Holding Your Breath:** Bracing does not mean stopping your breath. Keep breathing through the hold.

---

## Tips

1. **Squeeze Your Glutes:** Tightening the glutes tips the pelvis slightly and makes it much easier to keep the lower back from sagging.
2. **Look at the Floor:** Keeping your head in line with your spine, eyes on the floor just ahead of your hands, keeps your neck out of it.
3. **Quality Beats Time:** A strict 20-second hold does more than a sagging 90-second one. Add time only once the shape holds.
4. **Stop if Your Lower Back Hurts:** ACE's instructions say to stop the exercise and speak to a doctor if you feel low back pain during it.

## Modifications (Easier/Harder)

| Type | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 🔽 Easier | Knee Plank | Resting on your knees instead of your toes shortens the lever and cuts the load. |
| 🔽 Easier | Incline Plank (Hands on a Bench) | Putting your forearms on a bench raises your upper body and makes the hold lighter. |
| 🔼 Harder | Weighted Plank | A plate placed on your upper back adds load without adding time. |
| 🔼 Harder | Long-Lever Plank | Walking your elbows further forward lengthens the lever and makes the same hold much harder. |

## FAQs

1. **Q: How long should I hold a plank?**
   A: Long enough to keep a straight line, usually 20 to 45 seconds. ACE's instructions call for at least 5 seconds as a minimum. Once a hold stays clean past about a minute, add weight instead of more time.
2. **Q: Where should my elbows be?**
   A: Directly under your shoulders and close to your sides, with your palms flat and your hands facing forward.
3. **Q: Why does my lower back hurt in a plank?**
   A: Usually the hips are sagging, which passes the load to the lower back. Shorten the hold, squeeze your glutes, and stop if the pain continues — ACE advises speaking to a doctor about low back pain in this exercise.
4. **Q: Will planks give me a flat stomach?**
   A: They build the muscle that holds your trunk steady, but how visible your abs are depends on body fat, which comes down to diet and overall activity.
5. **Q: Plank or crunch?**
   A: They do different jobs. The plank trains the abs to stop the spine moving; the crunch trains them to bend it. The plans on this site use both.
