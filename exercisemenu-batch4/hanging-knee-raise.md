## Quick Answer (AEO)

The hanging knee raise is a bodyweight ab exercise. Hang from a bar with straight arms, bend your knees and lift your thighs to about hip height, then lower them slowly without swinging. Use 3 sets of 10 to 15 reps.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Iliopsoas and Rectus Femoris — the hip flexors; they lift the thighs, and they do most of the work in this exercise.
Rectus Abdominis — curls the pelvis at the top of the rep and stops the lower back arching on the way down.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
External and Internal Obliques — keep the hips from swinging side to side.
Latissimus Dorsi and Teres Major — hold the upper arms steady against the pull of the hang.
Forearm Flexors — hold your grip on the bar.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hang from the bar | Take an overhand grip about shoulder-width apart and hang with your arms straight and your legs together. |
| 2 | Settle the swing | Let your body stop moving before the first rep. Pull your shoulders down away from your ears so you are not hanging loose. |
| 3 | Lift your knees | Bend your knees and lift your thighs until they reach about hip height, as the demonstration shows. ACE describes the same movement: start with the knees bent and lift them slowly as high as possible, pulling the knees toward the chest. Breathe out as you lift. |
| 4 | Curl at the top | At the top, tilt your pelvis back slightly so your lower abs finish the rep rather than only your hip flexors. Pause for a second. |
| 5 | Lower slowly | Lower over about 2 seconds until your legs hang straight again, keeping your body still, then repeat. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Swinging Between Reps:** Using a backward swing to throw the knees up turns it into momentum work. Come to a dead stop before you start, and reset if you begin to swing.
2. **Stopping Too Low:** Lifting the knees only a few inches barely moves the hips. Bring the thighs to about hip height or higher.
3. **Letting the Legs Drop:** Releasing at the top and letting the legs fall skips the part of the rep where the abs have to stop the lower back arching.
4. **Only Lifting, Never Curling:** If the pelvis never tilts back at the top, the hip flexors do nearly all the work. Add that small curl.
5. **Gripping Until the Abs Are Untrained:** If your hands give out first, use straps so the set ends when your abs are done, not your grip.

---

## Tips

1. **Not for Everyone's Lower Back:** ACE notes that exercises where the legs hang unsupported are good for the deep abdominal muscles, but are not recommended for people with diagnosed lower-back pain.
2. **Start on a Captain's Chair:** ACE suggests the captain's chair — the padded forearm station — as the beginner version, because your back is supported and your grip is not the limit.
3. **Think Hips, Not Feet:** Aiming to lift your hip bones toward your ribs gives a better rep than aiming to lift your feet.
4. **Add Weight Before Adding Reps:** Once 15 clean reps are easy, hold a light dumbbell between your feet rather than pushing past 20.

## FAQs

1. **Q: Knee raise or straight-leg raise?**
   A: The knee raise is the easier of the two and is what this demonstration shows. Straight legs make the same movement much harder, so most people work up to it.
2. **Q: Why do I feel it in my hip flexors and not my abs?**
   A: The hip flexors lift the thighs, so some of that is normal. To bring the abs in, tilt your pelvis back at the top so the rep finishes with a small curl.
3. **Q: How do I stop swinging?**
   A: Come to a complete stop before the first rep, lower slowly rather than dropping, and stop the set once a swing creeps in.
4. **Q: Is this safe for my lower back?**
   A: For most people, yes. ACE advises against unsupported hanging leg exercises for anyone with diagnosed lower-back pain, so use the captain's chair or a lying version instead if that applies to you.
5. **Q: How often should I train abs?**
   A: The plans on this site train them once or twice a week, 2–3 sets each time.
