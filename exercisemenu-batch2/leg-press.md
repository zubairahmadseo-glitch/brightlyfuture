## Quick Answer (AEO)

The leg press is a machine exercise for the quads and glutes. Sit with your back flat on the pad and feet about hip-width on the platform, lower the sled until your knees bend to about 90 degrees without your hips lifting, then press back up without locking your knees. Use 3 sets of 10 to 15 reps.

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Quadriceps — straighten the knees to push the platform away.
Gluteus Maximus — extends the hips, especially from the deepest position.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Adductor Magnus — assists hip extension.
Hamstrings — help extend the hips and steady the knees.
Calves (Gastrocnemius and Soleus) — keep the feet steady on the platform.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sit in the machine | Sit with your back and hips flat against the pad and your head resting back. |
| 2 | Set your feet | Place your feet about hip- to shoulder-width apart in the middle of the platform, toes pointing slightly out. |
| 3 | Release the safeties | Press the platform up until your legs are almost straight, then turn the safety handles to release the sled. Hold the side handles. |
| 4 | Lower with control | Bend your knees to lower the sled toward your chest until your knees reach about 90 degrees, or as deep as you can while your hips and lower back stay on the pad. |
| 5 | Press back up | Push through your whole foot to press the platform back up. Stop just before your knees lock. |
| 6 | Lock the safeties | After the last rep, turn the safety handles back into place before you take your feet off the platform. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Hips Lifting off the Pad:** Lowering too deep makes your hips curl up and your lower back round. Stop where your hips and back stay flat on the pad.
2. **Locking Your Knees:** Snapping your knees straight at the top puts stress on the joint. Stop just short of lockout.
3. **Knees Caving In:** Letting your knees fall inward reduces control. Keep them in line with your toes.
4. **Heels Lifting:** Pushing through your toes shifts stress to the knees. Keep your whole foot flat and press through your heels and mid-foot.
5. **Bouncing at the Bottom:** Dropping the sled fast and bouncing out uses momentum. Lower under control and press smoothly.

## Tips

1. **Foot Position: The Evidence Is Mixed:** A 2020 review by Martín-Fuentes and colleagues found mixed results on foot position. One study saw more glute activity with a high foot position and more quad activity with a low one, but the authors recommend a self-selected position.
2. **Depth Comes Before Weight:** Use a load you can lower to your full, safe depth. Short reps with heavy weight train less of the movement.
3. **Use the Safeties Every Set:** Lock the safety handles before you get in and after you finish.
4. **Keep Your Head Back:** Resting your head on the pad helps keep your back flat.

## Modifications (Easier/Harder)

| Type | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 🔽 Easier | Bodyweight Box Squat | Squatting down to a box teaches the same knee and hip bend with no added load. |
| 🔽 Easier | Leg Press with Shorter Range | Lowering only as far as feels controlled, then adding depth over time, is a simple way to start. |
| 🔼 Harder | Single-Leg Leg Press | Pressing with one leg at a time puts more load on each leg and shows any difference between sides. |
| 🔼 Harder | Paused Leg Press | Hold the bottom position for 2 seconds on every rep before you press. |

## FAQs

1. **Q: How deep should I go on the leg press?**
   A: As deep as you can while your hips and lower back stay on the pad, usually around 90 degrees of knee bend or a little more.
2. **Q: Where should I put my feet?**
   A: About hip- to shoulder-width in the middle of the platform. Research on high versus low foot position is mixed, so choose the position that feels strongest and comfortable.
3. **Q: Is the leg press as good as squats?**
   A: Both build the quads and glutes. The leg press needs less balance and technique, while squats also train your trunk to support the bar. Many programs use both.
4. **Q: Should I lock my knees at the top?**
   A: No. Stop just before your knees lock so the muscles keep working and the joint is not snapped straight under load.
5. **Q: Why does my lower back hurt on the leg press?**
   A: Usually because your hips lift and your back rounds at the bottom. Reduce the depth or the weight and keep your hips on the pad.
