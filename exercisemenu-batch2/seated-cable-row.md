## Quick Answer (AEO)

The seated cable row trains the lats and middle back. Sit with your feet on the plates and knees slightly bent, hold a V-bar with straight arms, then pull it to your upper stomach while sitting tall and squeezing your shoulder blades together. Return slowly. Use 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Latissimus Dorsi — pulls the upper arms back toward the body.
Rhomboids — pull the shoulder blades together at the end of the row.
Middle Trapezius — pulls the shoulder blades together and holds them steady.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Posterior Deltoid — assists in pulling the arms back.
Biceps Brachii and Brachialis — bend the elbows during the pull.
Erector Spinae — hold your back straight while you sit and row.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sit and set your feet | Sit on the bench and place your feet on the foot plates with your knees slightly bent. |
| 2 | Grab the V-bar | Lean forward from your hips with a flat back and grab the V-bar, palms facing each other. |
| 3 | Start with arms reaching | Hold the handle with straight arms, letting your shoulders reach forward and your torso lean slightly forward, back still flat. |
| 4 | Row to your upper stomach | Sit tall as you pull the handle to your upper stomach, keeping your elbows close to your body. Squeeze your shoulder blades together and pause for a second. |
| 5 | Return slowly | Straighten your arms over 2–3 seconds and let your shoulders reach forward again, then repeat. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Leaning Back to Pull:** Throwing your torso backward uses body weight and stresses the lower back. Stop when you are sitting upright and let your arms and back finish the pull.
2. **Rounding the Lower Back:** Reaching forward is fine, but the movement should come from your hips with your back flat, not from slumping.
3. **Pulling Only with the Arms:** Bending your elbows without moving your shoulder blades leaves the back underworked. Finish each rep by squeezing your shoulder blades together.
4. **Shrugging:** Lifting your shoulders toward your ears shifts work to the upper traps. Keep your shoulders down.
5. **Letting the Stack Snap Back:** Releasing the handle fast loses control. Return over 2–3 seconds.

---

## Tips

1. **Avoid a Backward Lean:** ACE advises avoiding any backward lean during the pull to reduce stress on the lower back.
2. **Think Elbows Back:** Driving your elbows past your sides keeps the work in your back rather than your arms.
3. **Use the Stretch:** Letting your shoulders reach forward at the start, with a flat back, trains the back through a fuller range.
4. **Try Other Handles:** A wide bar shifts more work to the upper back; a V-bar keeps the elbows close and the lats working.

## FAQs

1. **Q: Should I lean forward at the start?**
   A: A small lean from the hips with a flat back is fine and lets your shoulders reach forward. Sit upright as you pull; don't lean back past vertical.
2. **Q: Where should I pull the handle to?**
   A: To your upper stomach or lower ribs, with your elbows close to your body.
3. **Q: V-bar or wide bar?**
   A: Both train the back. A V-bar keeps your elbows close to your sides; a wide bar spreads your elbows and works the upper back more.
4. **Q: Seated cable row or bent-over row?**
   A: The seated cable row supports your lower body and keeps tension steady, while the bent-over row also trains you to hold a hip hinge. Many programs use both.
5. **Q: Why does my lower back hurt?**
   A: Usually from rounding your lower back or leaning far back to move the weight. Lower the weight and keep your back flat.
