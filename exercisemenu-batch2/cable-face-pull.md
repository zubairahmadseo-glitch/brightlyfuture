## Quick Answer (AEO)

The cable face pull trains the rear delts and upper back. Set a rope at about shoulder to face height, keep your elbows high, and pull the rope toward your face while separating the ends beside your ears. Return slowly. Use a light weight for 3 sets of 12 to 20 reps.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Posterior Deltoid — the rear of the shoulder, pulls the upper arms back.
Middle Trapezius — pulls the shoulder blades together.
Rhomboids — pull the shoulder blades together and hold them in place.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Infraspinatus and Teres Minor — rotator cuff muscles that turn the upper arm outward at the end of the pull.
Lower Trapezius — helps control the shoulder blades.
Biceps Brachii and Brachialis — bend the elbows during the pull.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set the pulley | Attach a rope to a cable pulley and set it at about shoulder to face height. |
| 2 | Take your stance | Hold one end of the rope in each hand and step back until your arms are straight and the weight lifts off the stack. Stand tall with your feet about hip-width apart. |
| 3 | Pull toward your face | Pull the middle of the rope toward your face, leading with your elbows. Keep your elbows high, at or slightly above shoulder height. |
| 4 | Separate the rope | As the rope nears your face, pull the ends apart so your hands finish beside your ears. Squeeze your shoulder blades together and pause for a second. |
| 5 | Return slowly | Let your arms straighten over about 2 seconds while keeping your torso still, then repeat. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Letting the Elbows Drop:** When the elbows fall below the shoulders, the face pull turns into a row and the rear delts do less. Keep your elbows high.
2. **Using Too Much Weight:** Heavy loads make you lean back and swing. Choose a weight you can pull to your face and hold for a second.
3. **Shrugging:** Lifting your shoulders toward your ears shifts work to the upper traps. Keep your shoulders down as you pull.
4. **Pulling to the Chest or Neck:** Aim the middle of the rope at your face, around nose to forehead level.
5. **Leaning Back:** Using your body weight to start the pull takes the load off the target muscles. Keep your torso still.

---

## Tips

1. **Finish with Your Hands Beside Your Ears:** Pulling the rope ends apart at the end of each rep makes the upper arms rotate outward.
2. **Pause at Your Face:** A one-second hold at the end of each rep stops you from using momentum.
3. **Use a Staggered Stance if Needed:** If the stack pulls you forward, put one foot slightly in front of the other.
4. **Think Light and Strict:** Face pulls are usually done with light weights and higher reps.

## FAQs

1. **Q: What muscles do face pulls work?**
   A: Mainly the rear delts, middle traps and rhomboids. The rotator cuff muscles that turn the arm outward work at the end of each rep.
2. **Q: How high should the cable be?**
   A: About shoulder to face height. That lets you keep your elbows high while you pull toward your face.
3. **Q: Why do programs add face pulls to bench press days?**
   A: Many programs use them to balance lots of pressing with direct work for the rear delts and upper back.
4. **Q: Face pull or reverse fly — which is better?**
   A: Both train the rear delts. The face pull adds outward rotation of the shoulder at the end of each rep, which a reverse fly does not.
5. **Q: Can I use a band instead of a cable?**
   A: Yes. A band anchored at face height lets you do the same movement at home.
