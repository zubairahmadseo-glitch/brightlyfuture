## Quick Answer (AEO)

The barbell incline bench press is a compound lift that trains the upper chest, front shoulders, and triceps. Set the bench to about 30 degrees, grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder-width, lower it to your upper chest, then press it back up over your shoulders. Start with 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps.

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Pectoralis Major (Clavicular Head) — the upper chest, drives the press on an incline.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Anterior Deltoid — the front of the shoulder, works more on an incline than on a flat bench.
Triceps Brachii — straighten the elbows to lock out the bar.
Pectoralis Major (Sternal Head) — the mid chest, still assists the press.
Serratus Anterior — helps stabilize the shoulder blades.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set the bench to about 30 degrees | Lie on the incline bench with your eyes just in front of the bar and your feet flat on the floor. |
| 2 | Grip and set your shoulders | Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder-width. Pull your shoulder blades back and down into the bench. |
| 3 | Unrack | Lift the bar out of the rack and hold it with straight arms over your shoulders. |
| 4 | Lower to the upper chest | Lower the bar under control to your upper chest, just below the collarbones, with elbows about 45–70 degrees from your torso. |
| 5 | Press back up | Press the bar up until your arms lock out with the bar over your shoulders. |
| 6 | Re-rack | After the last rep, guide the bar back into the rack before relaxing your arms. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Setting the Bench Too Steep:** Angles above 45 degrees shift more of the work to the front shoulders. Keep the bench near 30 degrees.
2. **Bouncing the Bar off the Chest:** Rebounding uses momentum instead of muscle and is hard to control. Touch the chest lightly, then press.
3. **Flaring the Elbows to 90 Degrees:** Fully flared elbows put more stress on the shoulders. Keep them at a moderate angle from your body.
4. **Lifting the Hips off the Bench:** Driving the hips up flattens the incline and usually means the load is too heavy. Keep your glutes on the bench.
5. **Letting the Bar Drift Toward the Stomach:** The bar should finish over your shoulders. Pressing it forward puts the weight in a weaker position.

## Tips

1. **Check the Bench Angle:** Not every bench marks its angle. Aim for about 30 degrees and use the same setting every week so you can track progress.
2. **Keep the Shoulder Blades Pinned:** Holding them back and down keeps the chest working and the shoulders in a stable position.
3. **Control the Lowering:** Lower the bar over about 2 seconds to keep tension on the upper chest.
4. **Use Safety Arms or a Spotter:** Set the safety arms just below your lowest bar position, or train with a spotter.

## FAQs

1. **Q: What bench angle is best for the upper chest?**
   A: About 30 degrees. A 2020 study by Rodríguez-Ridao and colleagues found upper chest activity peaked at 30 degrees, while angles above 45 degrees shifted more work to the front shoulders.
2. **Q: Should I do incline bench before or after flat bench?**
   A: Do first the press you most want to improve. If upper chest is your priority, start with the incline.
3. **Q: Can beginners do the incline bench press?**
   A: Yes, with light loads once they can bench press with good form. Many beginners learn the pattern first with dumbbells.
4. **Q: How often should I train incline bench?**
   A: Once or twice a week, usually on a push or upper-body day, is common.
5. **Q: Why can I lift less on an incline than on a flat bench?**
   A: The front shoulders take a bigger share of the work on an incline, so most lifters use less weight than on a flat bench.
