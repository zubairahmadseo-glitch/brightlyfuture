## Quick Answer (AEO)

The barbell bench press is a compound lift that trains the chest, front shoulders, and triceps. Lie on a flat bench, grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder-width, lower it to your mid-chest with elbows tucked at a moderate angle, then press it back up. Start with 3 sets of 8 to 10 reps.

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Pectoralis Major — the chest, produces most of the pressing force.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Anterior Deltoid — the front of the shoulder, assists the press.
Triceps Brachii — straighten the elbows to lock out the bar.
Serratus Anterior — helps stabilize the shoulder blades.
Rotator Cuff — keeps the shoulder joint stable.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set up under the bar | Lie on the bench with your eyes under the bar and your feet flat on the floor. |
| 2 | Set your grip | Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder-width, with your wrists stacked over your forearms. |
| 3 | Pin your shoulder blades | Pull your shoulder blades back and down into the bench, keeping a small natural arch in your lower back. |
| 4 | Unrack | Lift the bar out of the rack and hold it with straight arms over your shoulders. |
| 5 | Lower to mid-chest | Lower the bar under control to your mid or lower chest, with elbows about 45–70 degrees from your torso. |
| 6 | Press up | Press the bar up and slightly back until your arms lock out over your shoulders. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Bouncing the Bar off the Chest:** Rebounding uses momentum and is hard to control. Touch the chest lightly, then press.
2. **Flaring the Elbows to 90 Degrees:** Fully flared elbows put the shoulders in a more stressful position. Keep them at a moderate angle from your body.
3. **Lifting the Hips off the Bench:** Driving the hips up changes the pressing angle and usually means the load is too heavy. Keep your glutes on the bench.
4. **Losing the Shoulder Blade Position:** If the shoulder blades slide forward during the set, the shoulders take over. Set them before you unrack and hold them for every rep.
5. **Grip Too Wide or Too Narrow:** An extra-wide grip can strain the shoulders, and a narrow grip shifts work to the triceps. Start slightly wider than shoulder-width.

---

## Tips

1. **Set Your Feet First:** Planting both feet firmly gives you a stable base and keeps your hips on the bench.
2. **Think About Bending the Bar:** Trying to bend the bar apart with your hands helps keep your shoulder blades and elbows in position.
3. **Lower Slower Than You Press:** Take about 2 seconds on the way down, then press with intent.
4. **Use Safety Arms or a Spotter:** Set the safety arms just below chest height, or ask for a spotter, so you can train close to failure safely.

## FAQs

1. **Q: How much should a beginner bench press?**
   A: Start with a weight you can lift for 8–10 controlled reps. For many beginners this is the empty 20 kg (45 lb) bar or a lighter fixed barbell. Add 1–2.5 kg (2.5–5 lb) when every set feels solid.
2. **Q: Should I touch my chest on every rep?**
   A: Yes, unless a shoulder problem stops you. A full range of motion trains the chest through its whole range and gives you a consistent way to track strength.
3. **Q: How often should I bench press?**
   A: One to three times a week works for most lifters, depending on the rest of the program.
4. **Q: Is flat bench or incline better for the chest?**
   A: Use both. A 2020 study by Rodríguez-Ridao and colleagues found the mid and lower chest were most active on a flat bench and the upper chest at about 30 degrees.
5. **Q: Should I arch my back when benching?**
   A: A small natural arch is normal and helps hold the shoulder blades in place. A large arch shortens the range of motion and is mainly a powerlifting technique.

**Citation (FAQ 4):** Rodríguez-Ridao D, et al. *Effect of Five Bench Inclinations on the Electromyographic Activity of the Pectoralis Major, Anterior Deltoid, and Triceps Brachii during the Bench Press Exercise.* Int J Environ Res Public Health. 2020;17(19):7339. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7579505/
