## Quick Answer (AEO)

The barbell preacher curl is a biceps isolation exercise done with your upper arms resting on an angled pad, which stops body swing. Grip the bar underhand, curl it toward your shoulders, then lower it slowly until your arms are almost straight. Use 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Biceps Brachii — bends the elbow and supinates the forearm, the main target of the curl.
Brachialis — a strong elbow flexor under the biceps that works hard in every curl.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Brachioradialis — a forearm muscle that assists elbow flexion.
Forearm Flexors — hold the grip on the bar.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set up on the bench | Adjust the preacher bench so the top of the pad sits in your armpits when your upper arms rest flat on it. |
| 2 | Grip the bar | Take an underhand grip about shoulder-width apart. Rest the backs of your upper arms fully on the pad. |
| 3 | Curl the bar up | Bend your elbows to curl the bar toward your shoulders. Keep your upper arms in contact with the pad the whole time. |
| 4 | Stop before the arms go vertical | Stop when your forearms are just short of vertical, where the biceps still hold tension, and squeeze briefly. |
| 5 | Lower slowly | Lower the bar over 2–3 seconds until your elbows are almost straight. Do not let the bar drop into the bottom. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Dropping into the Bottom:** Letting the bar fall to full extension puts sudden stress on the elbows and biceps tendon. Lower under control, especially in the last few inches.
2. **Lifting the Elbows off the Pad:** Raising the upper arms brings the shoulders into the lift and removes the main benefit of the pad. Keep the arms flat.
3. **Using Too Much Weight:** The pad makes this harder than a standing curl, so you will use less weight. Pick a load you can lower slowly.
4. **Pad Set at the Wrong Height:** A pad that is too low forces you to hunch over, and too high pushes the shoulders up. Adjust so the pad sits in your armpits.
5. **Bending the Wrists:** Curling the wrists toward you shifts work to the forearms. Keep the wrists straight.

---

## Tips

1. **Control the Stretch:** The bottom of the preacher curl is where the biceps are most stretched. Lowering slowly here is safer and keeps tension on the muscle.
2. **Use an EZ Bar for Wrist Comfort:** If a straight bar feels hard on your wrists, an EZ bar lets your hands sit at a slight angle.
3. **Stop Just Short of Lockout:** Ending each rep with a slight bend in the elbow keeps tension on the biceps and protects the joint.
4. **Program After Your Main Back Work:** Preacher curls fit at the end of a pull or arm session as a finishing exercise.

## FAQs

1. **Q: Is the preacher curl better than the standing curl?**
   A: It is different. The pad removes body swing and loads the biceps hardest when they are stretched, while standing curls are hardest in the middle of the rep.
2. **Q: Straight bar or EZ bar on the preacher bench?**
   A: Both work. An EZ bar is usually more comfortable on the wrists, which is why it is common on preacher benches.
3. **Q: Are preacher curls bad for the elbows?**
   A: Not with a controlled lowering phase and a sensible load. Dropping fast into the bottom with heavy weight is what puts the elbows at risk.
4. **Q: Should I fully straighten my arms?**
   A: Lower until your arms are almost straight, keeping a slight bend so the biceps stay loaded and the elbow is protected.
5. **Q: Why can I lift less on preacher curls?**
   A: The pad stops you from using momentum, and the biceps work hardest in the stretched position, so the same weight feels heavier.
