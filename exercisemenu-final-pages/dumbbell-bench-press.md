## Quick Answer (AEO)

The dumbbell bench press is a compound chest exercise done on a flat bench. Each arm presses its own dumbbell, which allows a slightly deeper lowering than a barbell. It trains the chest, front shoulders, and triceps. Use 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps for muscle growth.

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Pectoralis Major — the chest, both the upper (clavicular) and mid (sternal) fibers press the dumbbells.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Anterior Deltoid — the front of the shoulder, assists the press.
Triceps Brachii — straighten the elbows at the top.
Serratus Anterior — helps stabilize the shoulder blades.
Biceps Brachii — helps steady the dumbbells.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Get the dumbbells into position | Sit on the end of the bench with a dumbbell on each thigh. Lie back and use your thighs to help bring the dumbbells up to chest level. |
| 2 | Set your base | Plant your feet on the floor and pull your shoulder blades back and down against the bench. |
| 3 | Start at chest level | Hold the dumbbells beside your chest, palms facing forward, elbows about 45–75 degrees from your torso. |
| 4 | Press up | Press the dumbbells up in a slight arc until your arms are straight over your chest. Do not bang them together. |
| 5 | Lower with control | Lower the dumbbells over 2–3 seconds to the sides of your chest, as deep as your shoulders comfortably allow. |
| 6 | Finish safely | After the last rep, bring the dumbbells to your thighs as you sit up. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Flaring the Elbows to 90 Degrees:** Fully flared elbows put the front of the shoulder in a more stressful position. Keep them about 45–75 degrees from your torso.
2. **Cutting the Range Short:** Stopping high reduces the stretch on the chest, one of the main benefits of dumbbells. Lower until the dumbbells reach chest level or slightly below.
3. **Shoulders Rolling Forward at the Top:** Letting the shoulder blades lift off the bench takes tension off the chest. Keep them back and down.
4. **Going Too Heavy Too Soon:** Dumbbells need more balance than a barbell. Start lighter than you expect and add weight once every rep is steady.
5. **Dropping the Dumbbells to the Side:** Letting heavy dumbbells fall out wide with the arms stretched can strain the shoulders. Bring them to your thighs and sit up.

---

## Tips

1. **Train Both Sides Evenly:** Each arm lifts its own weight, so a weaker side cannot hide behind a stronger one.
2. **Keep a Moderate Elbow Angle:** If your shoulders tire before your chest, your elbows are probably too wide.
3. **Know What Dumbbells Change:** Saeterbakken and colleagues (2011) found similar chest and front-shoulder activity in barbell, Smith machine, and dumbbell bench presses, but 1-RM was about 17% lower with dumbbells.
4. **Do It Early in the Session:** Press while you are fresh, and balance your weekly pressing with rows.

## Modifications (Easier/Harder)

| Type | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 🔽 Easier | Dumbbell Floor Press | Pressing from the floor shortens the range at the bottom and removes the need to get off a bench with heavy weights. |
| 🔽 Easier | Machine Chest Press | A guided path removes the balance demand of two dumbbells. |
| 🔼 Harder | Dumbbell Incline Bench Press | A bench at about 30 degrees shifts more of the work to the upper chest and front shoulders. |
| 🔼 Harder | Dumbbell Bench Press with Slow Lowering | Lowering each rep over 3–4 seconds makes the same weight harder and builds control. |

## FAQs

1. **Q: How is the dumbbell bench press different from the barbell bench press?**
   A: Each arm works on its own, and the dumbbells can travel slightly lower than a bar. A barbell usually lets you lift more total weight.
2. **Q: Should the dumbbells touch at the top?**
   A: They can come close, but there is no need to bang them together. Keep the movement controlled.
3. **Q: Why do my shoulders hurt during dumbbell bench press?**
   A: Flared elbows, shoulders rolling forward, or lowering too deep for your mobility are common causes. Adjust these first, and see a clinician if pain continues.
4. **Q: What weight should a beginner use?**
   A: A weight you can press for 10–12 controlled reps with a full range of motion. The right starting weight varies widely from person to person.
5. **Q: Can I do it without a bench?**
   A: Yes. The dumbbell floor press trains the same pattern with a shorter range of motion.
