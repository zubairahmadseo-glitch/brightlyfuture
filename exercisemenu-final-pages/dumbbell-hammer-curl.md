## Quick Answer (AEO)

The dumbbell hammer curl is an elbow flexion exercise done with a neutral grip, thumbs pointing up. It works the brachialis and brachioradialis more than a palms-up curl, while the biceps still help. Curl one arm at a time or both together. Use 3 to 4 sets of 10 to 15 reps.

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Brachialis — a strong elbow flexor under the biceps.
Brachioradialis — the forearm muscle along the thumb side, works hard with a neutral grip.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Biceps Brachii — still bends the elbow, but less than in a palms-up curl.
Forearm Flexors — hold the dumbbell.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stand with a neutral grip | Stand with feet hip-width apart, a dumbbell in each hand at your sides, palms facing your body and thumbs forward. |
| 2 | Fix your elbows | Keep your elbows close to your sides. They should not drift forward or out. |
| 3 | Curl one dumbbell | Curl one dumbbell up toward your shoulder, keeping your thumb pointing up the whole time. |
| 4 | Pause at the top | Hold briefly when your forearm is about vertical, without moving the elbow forward. |
| 5 | Lower and switch | Lower the dumbbell over 2–3 seconds, then repeat with the other arm. You can also curl both arms together. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Turning the Palm Up Mid-Curl:** Rotating the wrist turns the exercise into a standard curl. Keep the thumbs up from bottom to top.
2. **Swinging the Dumbbells Forward:** Letting the weights swing in front of you uses momentum. Keep them moving straight up and down at your sides.
3. **Elbows Drifting Forward:** Moving the elbows forward lets the shoulders help. Keep them pinned to your sides.
4. **Rocking the Body:** If your torso rocks back on each rep, the weight is too heavy. Keep your body still.
5. **Rushing the Reps:** Fast, short reps reduce control. Lower each rep over 2–3 seconds.

---

## Tips

1. **Keep the Thumbs Up the Whole Time:** Holding the neutral grip from bottom to top keeps the brachialis and brachioradialis loaded.
2. **Pair Them with Palms-Up Curls:** Palms-up curls bias the biceps, and hammer curls bias the brachialis and brachioradialis, so using both trains all three elbow flexors.
3. **Try Cross-Body Hammer Curls:** Curling toward the opposite shoulder is a common variation that some lifters find more comfortable.
4. **Add Weight in Small Steps:** Move up by the smallest dumbbell increase available, usually 1–2 kg (2.5–5 lb), once all sets feel solid.

## FAQs

1. **Q: What is the difference between a hammer curl and a biceps curl?**
   A: The grip. A biceps curl uses palms up and biases the biceps; a hammer curl uses thumbs up and shifts more work to the brachialis and brachioradialis.
2. **Q: Should I alternate arms or curl both together?**
   A: Both are fine. Alternating gives each arm a short rest and makes strict form easier; curling both together saves time.
3. **Q: Why can I lift more on hammer curls?**
   A: With a neutral grip, the brachioradialis is in a strong position and the brachialis and biceps still help, so most people can lift a little more than with a palms-up curl.
4. **Q: Do hammer curls build the forearms?**
   A: Yes. They train the brachioradialis, the muscle along the thumb side of the forearm.
5. **Q: Can hammer curls replace biceps curls?**
   A: Not completely. Hammer curls load the biceps less than palms-up curls, so most programs include both.
