## Quick Answer (AEO)

The barbell standing calf raise is a calf exercise done standing with a barbell across your upper back. Straight knees make the gastrocnemius the main mover, with the soleus assisting. Raise your heels as high as you can, then lower them for a full stretch. Use 3 to 4 sets of 10 to 15 reps.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Gastrocnemius — the two-headed upper calf muscle, raises the heels and works hardest with straight knees.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Soleus — the deeper calf muscle, also raises the heels in every calf raise.
Tibialis Posterior — assists and stabilizes the ankle.
Peroneus Longus and Brevis — stabilize the ankle on the outside of the lower leg.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set the bar in the rack | Set the bar at about armpit height and rest it across your upper traps, as you would for a back squat. |
| 2 | Stand on a platform | Unrack the bar and place the balls of your feet on a step or weight plate, heels hanging off, feet about hip-width apart. |
| 3 | Keep your knees nearly straight | Brace your core and keep a slight, fixed bend in your knees for the whole set. |
| 4 | Lower into a stretch | Lower your heels below the platform until you feel a full stretch in your calves. |
| 5 | Rise as high as you can | Push through the balls of your feet to lift your heels as high as possible and pause for one second. |
| 6 | Lower with control | Lower your heels slowly over 2–3 seconds back to the stretched position. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Short, Bouncing Reps:** Bouncing in the middle of the range skips the stretch at the bottom and the full rise at the top. Move through the full range on every rep.
2. **Standing Flat on the Floor:** A flat floor stops the heels at floor level. A step or plate lets the heels drop below the toes for a longer stretch.
3. **Bending the Knees to Help:** Bending and straightening the knees turns the rep into a mini squat. Keep the knee angle fixed.
4. **Rolling onto the Outer Foot:** Letting the ankles roll outward shifts pressure off the big toe. Press evenly through the ball of the foot.
5. **Going Too Heavy:** If you cannot pause at the top or control the lowering, the weight is too heavy. Reduce it and restore the full range.

---

## Tips

1. **Pause at Both Ends:** A one-second pause at the top and a short pause in the stretch remove the bounce from the Achilles tendon so the calves do the work.
2. **Spend Time in the Stretch:** Kassiano and colleagues (2023) found calf raises trained in the stretched part of the range grew the medial gastrocnemius more than full-range or top-range raises.
3. **Keep the Knees Straight for the Gastrocnemius:** Cresswell and colleagues (1995) found gastrocnemius activity fell as the knee bent, while soleus activity stayed the same.
4. **Use a Smith Machine if Balance Limits You:** A fixed bar path lets you focus on the calves instead of balancing the bar.

## FAQs

1. **Q: Do I need a platform for standing calf raises?**
   A: It is strongly recommended. Standing on a step or plate lets the heels drop below the toes, which trains the calf through a longer, stretched range.
2. **Q: Standing or seated calf raises — which builds bigger calves?**
   A: Standing. In a 2023 study by Kinoshita and colleagues, standing raises grew the gastrocnemius by about 9–12%, compared with about 1–2% for seated raises, while soleus growth was similar in both.
3. **Q: Does pointing the toes in or out change which part of the calf grows?**
   A: Slightly. In a 9-week study by Nunes and colleagues (2020), toes pointed out favored the inner (medial) head and toes pointed in favored the outer (lateral) head.
4. **Q: How often should I train calves?**
   A: Two to three times a week works well for most people and makes it easy to spread your weekly calf sets.
5. **Q: What is the difference between the gastrocnemius and the soleus?**
   A: The gastrocnemius crosses the knee and ankle, so it works best with straight knees. The soleus lies underneath, crosses only the ankle, and works in every calf raise.
