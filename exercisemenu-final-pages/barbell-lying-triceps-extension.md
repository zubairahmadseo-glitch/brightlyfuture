## Quick Answer (AEO)

The barbell lying triceps extension is an isolation lift for all three heads of the triceps. Lie flat on a bench, hold a barbell over your shoulders with arms straight, and bend only at the elbows to lower it toward your forehead. Press back up. Use 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Triceps Brachii — all three heads (long, lateral, medial) straighten the elbow to lift the bar.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Anconeus — a small elbow extensor that assists the triceps.
Forearm Flexors — grip the bar and keep the wrists steady.
Latissimus Dorsi and Rear Shoulder — hold the upper arms still.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lie on the bench | Lie flat on a bench with your feet on the floor. Hold the barbell with an overhand grip, hands about shoulder-width apart. |
| 2 | Start with arms over your shoulders | Press the bar up so your arms are straight and vertical over your shoulders. |
| 3 | Fix your upper arms | Keep your elbows pointing up toward the ceiling and your upper arms still. Only the elbows will bend. |
| 4 | Lower toward your forehead | Bend your elbows to lower the bar slowly in an arc toward your forehead, stopping just above it. |
| 5 | Press back up | Straighten your elbows to raise the bar back to the start without letting your upper arms move. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Flaring the Elbows Outward:** Elbows that drift wide take work away from the triceps and strain the elbows. Keep them pointing up and about shoulder-width apart.
2. **Moving the Upper Arms:** Letting the upper arms swing turns the lift into a pullover. Keep them still so only the forearms move.
3. **Using Too Much Weight:** A load you cannot lower slowly is hard to control over your face and stresses the elbows. Pick a weight you can lower over 2–3 seconds.
4. **Dropping the Bar Fast:** Letting the bar fall toward your head is unsafe. Control every rep and stop just above the forehead.
5. **Lifting the Hips:** Raising the hips off the bench usually means the load is too heavy. Keep your glutes on the bench.

---

## Tips

1. **Use an EZ Bar if Your Wrists or Elbows Ache:** The angled grip lets the wrists turn slightly inward, which many lifters find more comfortable.
2. **Let the Upper Arms Tilt Back Slightly:** Letting the upper arms angle a little toward your head increases shoulder flexion, which puts the long head of the triceps under more stretch. Keep the movement at the elbows.
3. **Control the Lowering:** Take 2–3 seconds on the way down. A slow lowering keeps the bar safe and the triceps under tension.
4. **Use a Spotter for Heavier Sets:** The bar travels over your face, so ask for a spotter when you work close to failure.

## Modifications (Easier/Harder)

| Type | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 🔽 Easier | Dumbbell Lying Triceps Extension | Two dumbbells let each arm move on its own and allow a palms-facing grip that many lifters find easier on the elbows. |
| 🔽 Easier | Cable Overhead Triceps Extension | The cable keeps steady tension and removes the risk of a bar over your face. |
| 🔼 Harder | Incline Lying Triceps Extension | Doing the lift on a low incline bench angles the arms further overhead relative to the torso, which puts the long head of the triceps under a greater stretch. |
| 🔼 Harder | Close-Grip Bench Press Superset | Follow a set of lying extensions with a set of close-grip bench press to train the triceps with both an isolation and a compound lift. |

## FAQs

1. **Q: Is the barbell lying triceps extension the same as a skull crusher?**
   A: Yes. Skull crusher is the common gym name for this exercise. Some lifters lower the bar to the forehead and others just behind the head; both are the same lift.
2. **Q: Should I use a straight bar or an EZ bar?**
   A: Either works for the triceps. Many lifters find the angled EZ bar grip easier on the wrists and elbows, so it is a common choice.
3. **Q: Why do my elbows hurt during skull crushers?**
   A: Common causes are a load that is too heavy, lowering too fast, or flared elbows. Reduce the weight, slow the lowering, and try an EZ bar. See a clinician if pain continues.
4. **Q: Can I do this exercise standing or seated?**
   A: Not with the same bar path. Standing or seated versions become overhead triceps extensions, a related exercise with a different setup.
5. **Q: Is it safe for beginners?**
   A: Yes, with a light load and a controlled tempo. Because the bar moves over the face, many beginners start with dumbbells or a cable version.
