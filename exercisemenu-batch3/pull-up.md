## Quick Answer (AEO)

A pull-up is a bodyweight exercise for the lats and upper back. Hang from a bar with an overhand grip a little wider than your shoulders, pull your chest toward the bar by driving your elbows down, then lower until your arms are straight. Use 3 sets of 6 to 12 reps.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Latissimus Dorsi — pulls the upper arms down and back toward the body.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Teres Major — works with the lats to pull the arms down.
Lower and Middle Trapezius — pull the shoulder blades down and together.
Rhomboids — pull the shoulder blades together.
Biceps Brachii and Brachialis — bend the elbows during the pull.
Brachioradialis and Forearm Flexors — hold your grip on the bar.
Posterior Deltoid — assists in pulling the arms back.
Abdominals — keep your body from swinging.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set your grip | Reach up and take an overhand grip a little wider than shoulder-width, with your thumbs wrapped around the bar. |
| 2 | Hang with straight arms | Let yourself hang with your arms straight. Bend your knees and cross your ankles behind you, as shown, so your feet stay clear of the floor. |
| 3 | Set your shoulders | Pull your shoulders down away from your ears before the first rep so you are not hanging loose. |
| 4 | Pull up | Drive your elbows down toward your sides and pull your chest toward the bar until your chin clears it. Keep your body still. |
| 5 | Lower under control | Lower over 2–3 seconds until your arms are straight again, then start the next rep. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Swinging to Get Up:** Throwing the hips and legs to build momentum turns the pull-up into a different exercise. Keep your body still unless you are training kipping on purpose.
2. **Half Reps at the Bottom:** Stopping with your elbows still bent removes the hardest, most stretched part of the rep. Straighten your arms every time.
3. **Chin Over, Chest Away:** Craning your neck to clear the bar hides a short rep. Aim your chest at the bar instead.
4. **Shrugging at the Start:** Hanging with your shoulders up by your ears makes the first few inches harder than they need to be. Pull them down first.
5. **Dropping off the Bar:** Letting go at the top skips the lowering phase, which is a large part of the training effect.

## Tips

1. **The Pull-Up Works the Lower Traps Harder Than the Chin-Up:** Youdas and colleagues (2010) measured higher lower trapezius activity in the pull-up, while the chin-up produced more biceps and chest activity.
2. **Use a Band or the Assisted Machine to Build Up:** Both take part of your body weight, so you can train full-range reps before you can do one unassisted.
3. **Add Weight Once You Pass About 12 Reps:** A dip belt or a dumbbell held between your feet keeps your sets in a range that still builds strength.
4. **Train the Lowering First:** If you cannot pull up yet, step or jump to the top position and take 3–5 seconds to lower yourself. Build up to full reps from there.

## FAQs

1. **Q: Pull-up or chin-up?**
   A: The pull-up uses an overhand grip and the chin-up an underhand one. In Youdas and colleagues' 2010 study the chin-up produced more biceps and chest activity, while the pull-up produced more lower trapezius activity.
2. **Q: How wide should my grip be?**
   A: A little wider than shoulder-width, as in the demonstration. Much wider grips shorten the distance you travel, so most lifters stay close to this width.
3. **Q: I cannot do a single pull-up — where do I start?**
   A: Use band assistance or the assisted pull-up machine for full reps, and practise lowering yourself slowly from the top. Lat pulldowns build the same muscles while you work up.
4. **Q: Should I cross my ankles?**
   A: It is optional. Crossing them, as the demonstration shows, keeps your legs together and makes it easier to stop swinging.
5. **Q: Is the pull-up better than the lat pulldown?**
   A: They train the same muscles. The pull-up loads your full body weight, which suits lower reps, while the pulldown lets you pick any weight, which suits beginners and higher-rep sets.
