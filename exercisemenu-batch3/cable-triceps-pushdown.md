## Quick Answer (AEO)

The cable triceps pushdown trains the triceps. Face a high pulley, hold a rope with your elbows bent and tucked at your sides, then straighten your arms down until they are almost straight. Let the rope rise back slowly. Use 3 sets of 10 to 15 reps.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Triceps Brachii (Lateral and Medial Heads) — straighten the elbow.
Triceps Brachii (Long Head) — also straightens the elbow, but it is only lightly stretched when the arm hangs at your side.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Anconeus — assists at the end of elbow straightening.
Latissimus Dorsi and Posterior Deltoid — hold the upper arms down at your sides.
Forearm Flexors — hold your grip on the rope.
Abdominals — keep your torso still as you push.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set the pulley high | Attach a rope to a cable pulley set at or above head height. |
| 2 | Take your stance | Face the machine and stand about a step back, feet about hip-width apart, standing tall. |
| 3 | Set your elbows | Hold one end of the rope in each hand, palms facing each other, and pull your elbows in to your sides. Start with your hands at about upper-stomach height and your elbows bent. |
| 4 | Push down | Breathe out and straighten your arms down until they are almost straight. Your upper arms stay at your sides and only your forearms move. |
| 5 | Return slowly | Let the rope rise over 2–3 seconds until your elbows are bent again, then repeat without resting the stack. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Elbows Drifting Forward:** If your elbows travel away from your sides, your shoulders and lats start pushing. Keep the upper arms still against your ribs.
2. **Leaning Over the Cable:** Bending forward and pressing down with body weight moves the work away from the arms. Stand tall and keep your torso still.
3. **Letting the Weight Pull You Up:** Allowing the stack to snap your hands back up skips the lowering half of the rep. Control the way up over 2–3 seconds.
4. **Shrugging:** Lifting your shoulders toward your ears as you push shifts the work to the traps. Keep your shoulders down.
5. **Too Much Weight:** A heavy stack makes you rock, lean and bend the wrists. Pick a load you can push down with still upper arms.

---

## Tips

1. **Rope or Bar Makes Little Difference to the Triceps:** Rendos and colleagues (2016) compared handle types during standing pushdowns and found no significant difference in triceps activity between them. Use whichever your elbows and wrists prefer.
2. **Pair It With an Overhead Exercise:** Maeo and colleagues (2023) found the long head grew more when trained overhead than with the arm at the side. The pushdown is the arm-at-the-side position, so it works well alongside an overhead extension rather than instead of it.
3. **Keep Your Wrists Straight:** Let your wrists stay in line with your forearms rather than curling the rope down with your hands.
4. **Split the Rope at the Bottom:** Pulling the two ends slightly apart as your arms straighten makes the end of the rep easier to hold.

## FAQs

1. **Q: Rope or straight bar for pushdowns?**
   A: For the triceps it makes little difference. Rendos and colleagues (2016) measured no significant difference in triceps activity across handle types, so choose the one that feels best on your elbows and wrists.
2. **Q: How high should the pulley be?**
   A: At or above head height, so the cable pulls up and forward and your elbows can stay at your sides through the whole rep.
3. **Q: Why do I feel pushdowns in my shoulders?**
   A: Usually the elbows are drifting forward or you are leaning over the cable. Stand tall, pin the upper arms to your ribs and lower the weight.
4. **Q: Pushdown or overhead extension?**
   A: Both train the triceps in different arm positions. Maeo and colleagues (2023) found more long-head growth from the overhead position, so many programs run both, as the plans on this site do.
5. **Q: Should I lock my elbows at the bottom?**
   A: Straighten your arms until they are almost straight and hold for a moment. Snapping into a hard lockout under load adds nothing useful.

**Citation (Tip 1 & FAQ 1):** Rendos NK, Heredia Vargas HM, Alipio TC, Regis RC, Romero MA, Signorile JF. *Differences in Muscle Activity During Cable Resistance Training Are Influenced by Variations in Handle Types.* J Strength Cond Res. 2016;30(7):2001–2009. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26694502/

**Citation (Tip 2 & FAQ 4):** Maeo S, Wu Y, Huang M, et al. *Triceps brachii hypertrophy is substantially greater after elbow extension training performed in the overhead versus neutral arm position.* Eur J Sport Sci. 2023;23(7):1240–1250. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35819335/
