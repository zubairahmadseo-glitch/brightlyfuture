## Quick Answer (AEO)

The leg extension trains the quadriceps. Sit with your back on the pad and your knees lined up with the machine's pivot, set the roller just above your feet, then straighten your knees until your legs are almost straight. Lower slowly. Use 3 sets of 10 to 15 reps.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Rectus Femoris — the middle of the four quadriceps; straightens the knee and also crosses the hip.
Vastus Lateralis — the outer quadriceps; straightens the knee.
Vastus Medialis — the inner quadriceps; straightens the knee.
Vastus Intermedius — sits under the rectus femoris and straightens the knee.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Tibialis Anterior — holds the foot and ankle steady against the roller.
Abdominals and Hip Flexors — keep you sitting back in the seat.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set the back pad | Adjust the back pad so that when you sit right back, your knees line up with the pivot point of the machine arm. |
| 2 | Set the roller | Set the roller so it rests across the front of your lower legs, just above your feet. |
| 3 | Sit and take the handles | Sit with your back flat against the pad and hold the handles at the sides of the seat to stop yourself sliding forward. |
| 4 | Straighten your knees | Breathe out and straighten your legs until they are almost straight. Pause for a second at the top. |
| 5 | Lower slowly | Bend your knees over 2–3 seconds back to about 90 degrees, then repeat without letting the stack rest. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Knees Off the Pivot:** If your knee joint does not line up with the machine's pivot, the roller slides on your shin and the movement feels wrong. Reset the back pad before you start.
2. **Lifting Your Hips off the Seat:** Pushing your hips up to throw the weight takes the work off the quads. Hold the handles and keep your hips down.
3. **Swinging the Weight Up:** Kicking the roller and letting it drop skips most of the rep. Push up smoothly and take 2–3 seconds to lower.
4. **Roller on the Foot:** A roller sitting on the top of the foot rather than just above it is uncomfortable and harder to control.
5. **Cutting the Bottom Short:** Stopping before your knees get back to about 90 degrees trims off the part of the range where the quads are longest.

---

## Tips

1. **Do Not Cut the Deep Part of the Rep:** Pedrosa and colleagues (2022) trained untrained women on this machine in different ranges. The group that trained only the deep part of the range, from 100 to 65 degrees of knee bend, grew more in several parts of the muscle than the full-range group.
2. **You Cannot Target the Teardrop:** A systematic review of 20 EMG studies (Smith and colleagues, 2009) found that changing foot or leg position does not preferentially work the vastus medialis over the vastus lateralis. Turning your toes in or out will not shape the inner quad.
3. **Pause at the Top:** A one-second hold at the top stops you from bouncing out of each rep, and it lets you use less weight for the same effort.
4. **Hold the Handles:** Gripping the side handles keeps your hips in the seat, which keeps the work in your thighs.

## FAQs

1. **Q: Are leg extensions bad for your knees?**
   A: For most healthy people they are a normal part of training. If you have knee pain or a knee injury, use a lighter weight, work in the range that feels comfortable, and speak to a clinician before loading it heavily.
2. **Q: Does turning my toes in or out work different parts of the quad?**
   A: No. A review of 20 EMG studies by Smith and colleagues (2009) found that changing leg or foot position does not preferentially work the inner quad over the outer one.
3. **Q: Should I lock my knees at the top?**
   A: Straighten your legs until they are almost straight and hold for a second. Snapping the knees hard into lockout under a heavy load adds nothing useful.
4. **Q: Leg extension or squat?**
   A: They do different jobs. The squat trains the quads together with the glutes, hips and trunk, while the leg extension loads the quads on their own, which is useful as extra work after the heavy lifts.
5. **Q: How many sets should I do?**
   A: The plans on this site use 2–4 sets of 10–15 reps, usually at the end of a leg session after the squat or leg press.
