## Quick Answer (AEO)

The cable overhead triceps extension trains the triceps with the arms overhead. Face away from a low pulley, hold a rope behind your head with your elbows pointing forward, then straighten your arms overhead. Lower slowly. Use 3 sets of 10 to 15 reps.

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Triceps Brachii (Long Head) — crosses the shoulder as well as the elbow, so the overhead position stretches it before it straightens the elbow.
Triceps Brachii (Lateral and Medial Heads) — straighten the elbow.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Anconeus — assists at the end of elbow straightening.
Anterior Deltoid — helps hold the upper arms up and forward.
Abdominals and Erector Spinae — keep your torso upright against the pull of the cable.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set the pulley low | Attach a rope to the lowest setting on a cable machine and choose a light weight to start. |
| 2 | Pick the rope up | Face the machine, take one end of the rope in each hand, then turn away from the stack and bring your hands up and behind your head. |
| 3 | Take your stance | Stand tall with your feet about hip-width apart, as shown. Keep your ribs down so your lower back does not arch. |
| 4 | Set your elbows | Start with your elbows bent and pointing forward, close to your head, and your hands behind your neck. You should feel a stretch in the back of your arms. |
| 5 | Straighten your arms | Push the rope up and forward until your arms are straight overhead. Only your forearms move; the upper arms stay where they are. |
| 6 | Lower slowly | Bend your elbows over 2–3 seconds to bring your hands back behind your head, then repeat. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Elbows Flaring Out:** Letting the elbows drift wide turns the movement into an overhead press. Keep them pointing forward, close to your head.
2. **Moving the Upper Arms:** If the whole arm swings forward and back, the shoulders take over. Hold the upper arms still and move only the forearms.
3. **Arching the Lower Back:** Leaning back to finish the rep loads the spine instead of the arms. Keep your ribs down and your abs tight.
4. **Cutting the Stretch Short:** Stopping before your hands get behind your head removes the stretched position this exercise is chosen for.
5. **Loading It Like a Pushdown:** The overhead position handles less weight than an arms-at-the-side exercise. If the cable pulls you backward between reps, drop the load.

---

## Tips

1. **Overhead Builds More Than Arms-at-the-Side:** Maeo and colleagues (2023) trained one arm overhead and the other at the side for 12 weeks. The long head grew about 28% overhead against about 20% at the side, even though the overhead arm used 34–39% less weight.
2. **Chase the Stretch, Not the Load:** The overhead position is the point of this exercise, so take each rep to full depth behind your head.
3. **Keep Your Head Still:** Pushing your head forward to clear the rope changes your posture. Let the rope travel beside your head instead.
4. **Spread the Rope at the Top:** Pulling the two ends slightly apart as your arms straighten makes the finish easier to hold.

## FAQs

1. **Q: Does the overhead position build more triceps?**
   A: In a 12-week study by Maeo and colleagues (2023), the overhead-trained arm gained about 28% long-head volume against about 20% for the arm trained at the side, while using 34–39% less weight.
2. **Q: Low pulley or high pulley?**
   A: A low pulley behind you, as in the demonstration, keeps the cable pulling your hands down and back through the whole rep. A pulley set at head height also works. Keep your elbows pointing forward either way.
3. **Q: Rope or bar?**
   A: Both work. A rope lets your hands separate and your wrists turn freely at the top, while a straight or EZ bar fixes your grip.
4. **Q: Why do my elbows ache on this exercise?**
   A: Most often the weight is too heavy, or the elbows drift and snap straight at the top. Lower the load and stop just short of locking out. See a clinician if the pain carries on.
5. **Q: Overhead extension or pushdown — which should I pick?**
   A: They train the same muscle in different positions. The overhead version stretches the long head, and many programs use both; the workout plans on this site do exactly that.
