Meta Tag Best Practices for SEO in 2025

    
Follow these expert tips to maximise your search engine visibility.

  

  

    

      
1

      

        
Keep Title Tags Under 60 Characters

        
Google truncates titles longer than approximately 60 characters (or 580 pixels wide) in search results. Front-load your primary keyword in the title for maximum SEO impact. Include your brand name at the end separated by a pipe (|) or dash for brand recognition.

      

    

    

      
2

      

        
Write Meta Descriptions Between 120 and 160 Characters

        
Google displays approximately 155-160 characters of your meta description. Descriptions shorter than 120 characters may appear thin. Include a call-to-action and your target keyword naturally. Google may rewrite your description if it does not match the search query.

      

    

    

      
3

      

        
Always Set Open Graph Tags for Social Sharing

        
Without OG tags, social platforms guess your title, description, and image from page content. The results are often poor. Set og:title, og:description, og:image (1200x630 pixels minimum), and og:url explicitly for every page you want shared on social media.

      

    

    

      
4

      

        
Use Canonical Tags to Prevent Duplicate Content

        
If your page is accessible via multiple URLs (with and without www, with query parameters, or HTTP vs HTTPS), set a canonical URL to tell Google which version to index. This prevents diluted ranking signals across duplicate pages.

      

    

    

      
5

      

        
Set Unique Meta Tags for Every Page

        
Each page on your website should have a unique title tag and meta description that accurately describes its specific content. Duplicate meta tags across multiple pages confuse search engines and reduce your overall site visibility in search results.

Frequently Asked Questions About Meta Tags and SEO

      
Everything you need to know about creating effective meta tags.

    

    

      
Meta tags are HTML elements that provide information about your webpage to search engines and social media platforms. They do not appear on the visible page but exist in the source code inside the head section. The most important meta tags for SEO are the title tag (which directly affects rankings), meta description (which affects click-through rates from search results), canonical tags (which prevent duplicate content issues), and robots tags (which control how search engines crawl your site).

Google displays approximately 60 characters (or 580 pixels) of your title tag in search results. Titles longer than this will be truncated with an ellipsis. For best results, keep your title between 50 and 60 characters, place your primary keyword near the beginning, and include your brand name at the end. Our tool shows a real-time character counter with colour-coded feedback to help you stay within the optimal range.

      
Google typically displays between 155 and 160 characters of your meta description in search results. We recommend writing descriptions between 120 and 160 characters for optimal display. Descriptions shorter than 120 characters may appear thin and less compelling, while descriptions longer than 160 characters will be truncated. Include your target keyword naturally and add a call-to-action to encourage clicks.

      
Open Graph tags control how your webpage appears when shared on Facebook, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Slack, and other social platforms. Without OG tags, these platforms attempt to guess your title, description, and image from the page content, often producing poor results. If any page on your website gets shared on social media, OG tags are essential. The most important ones are og:title, og:description, og:image (1200x630 pixels recommended), og:url, and og:type.

      
Twitter Cards control how your links appear when shared on Twitter/X. The most common type is "Summary with Large Image" which displays a large image preview, title, and description. Use "Summary" for a smaller image card. "Player" is for video or audio content, and "App" is for mobile applications. For most websites and blog posts, "Summary with Large Image" provides the best engagement and click-through rates.

      
A canonical tag (rel="canonical") tells search engines which version of a page is the "master" copy when similar or identical content exists at multiple URLs. Common scenarios include: pages accessible with and without "www", HTTP and HTTPS versions, pages with tracking parameters (like ?utm_source), and paginated content. Setting a canonical URL prevents duplicate content issues and consolidates ranking signals to one URL.

      
The robots meta tag tells search engine crawlers how to handle your page. "index" allows the page to appear in search results. "follow" allows crawlers to follow links on the page. "noindex" prevents the page from appearing in search results (useful for thank-you pages, admin pages, or staging sites). "nofollow" prevents crawlers from following outbound links. "nosnippet" prevents Google from showing a text snippet in search results. "noarchive" prevents cached versions of your page.

      
Google officially stated in 2009 that they do not use the meta keywords tag as a ranking signal. Most major search engines have followed suit. However, some smaller search engines like Yandex may still consider them, and they can serve as an internal reference for your content strategy. We include the option in our generator for completeness, but it should not be your SEO priority. Focus on title tags, meta descriptions, and structured data instead.

      
There are several ways to add meta tags to WordPress. The easiest is using an SEO plugin like Yoast SEO or Rank Math which provide fields for title and description on each page. For custom meta tags (like OG tags or custom robots directives), you can paste the generated code into the plugin's custom head section, or add it via Appearance then Theme Header in your theme settings. Some themes also have built-in header code injection options.

This is useful because search and social audiences often respond to different messaging styles and calls-to-action.

For general meta tag inspection, right-click your page, select "View Page Source", and look inside the head section. Google Search Console also shows how Google interprets your title and description under the URL Inspection tool.

Always test your image using Facebook's Sharing Debugger before publishing.
