BrightlyFuture single post layout: what each numbered part does

  1. Breadcrumb (Home › Category › Post). Helps users and Google understand site structure; add BreadcrumbList schema (Rank Math does it).
  2. Category label + H1. One H1 only. Category links to the cluster's category page.
  3. Byline with E-E-A-T: author photo + name (links to author page), "Fact-checked" badge, published AND updated dates, reading time.
  4. Featured image 1200×800 WebP, with caption. Loads eagerly (it's the LCP element).
  5. Quick Answer box right under the image: the 45–50 word answer for featured snippets and AI Overviews.
  6. Disclosure line (affiliate / AI use) just before the content. Short, honest, links to Editorial Policy.
  7. Collapsible table of contents (mobile). On desktop the sticky sidebar TOC (12) does this job.
  8. Ad slot 1, after the intro, never above the Quick Answer. Reserve the height so the page doesn't jump (CLS).
  9. In-article tool box placed where the reader needs it, e.g. "Check your readability" inside a readability section.
  10. Proof block: real Search Console screenshot with caption. Your strongest Experience signal.
  11. Key takeaways + FAQ (FAQ answers not repeated from the body), then the author bio box and related posts from the same cluster.
  12. Cluster hub box (your ExerciseMenu "Training Hub" idea, improved): pillar first, every spoke listed, current article highlighted.
  13. Sticky sidebar: table of contents that highlights the current section + one tool + ad slot 2. Latest posts go low, not at the top.
  14. Mobile: sidebar drops below the article; hub box and TOC move to the top of the content area.

ExerciseMenu vs BrightlyFuture: what changes and why

  1. Hub box moves to the top of the sidebar (ExerciseMenu had it below Latest Posts, where few people see it). It's your most valuable internal link.
  2. Hub box lists every article in the cluster with the current one highlighted, not just a button. Readers see the path; Google sees the cluster.
  3. Latest posts move down. Random recent posts don't help the reader of this article; cluster links do.
  4. Sticky table of contents in the sidebar replaces empty space on long articles and keeps readers moving.
  5. Author byline adds role + "fact-checked" and keeps both dates (ExerciseMenu showed both; keep that). Add an author bio box at the end.
  6. "No Comments" in the byline adds nothing. Hide it until you have comments.
  7. Tooltip-style inline links (the boxed "medicine ball" link) look like ads to some readers. Use plain underlined links in the text.
  8. Ads: max 2–3 per post, never before the Quick Answer, always with reserved height. Content first is what AdSense reviewers want to see.

Technical checklist for every post

  1. Schema: Article (author = Person with sameAs links), BreadcrumbList, FAQPage. Rank Math handles all three.
  2. Speed: WebP images, width/height set, lazy-load everything below the featured image.
  3. One H1, then H2 → H3 in order. No skipped levels.
  4. Content width 680–760px for comfortable reading; body text 17–18px.
  5. URL: /blog/post-slug/ (set permalinks before publishing).
  6. Dates: change "Updated" only for real content changes.
  7. Mobile: TOC collapsible at top, sidebar below, no sticky ads covering text.
  8. Links: every post links to its pillar (hub box + in text), 2–3 related spokes and one tool.
