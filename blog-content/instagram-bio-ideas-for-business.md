# Instagram Bio Ideas for Business: 18 Examples You Can Adapt (2026)

> **Quick Answer:** A good Instagram bio for business says what you do and who it's for, gives one reason to trust you, adds the practical detail customers need (location, hours or delivery) and ends with one clear action. You have 150 characters, so every word should help a visitor decide to follow, tap or message.

[IMG 01-instagram-bio-featured.webp | Instagram bio ideas for business: 18 examples you can adapt in 2026]

Most bio guides show you what Nike or a celebrity wrote. That doesn't help a café in Leeds or a bookkeeper in Bristol, because those brands don't need to explain what they do. A small business does.

This guide gives you 18 bios for six types of business, a four-line formula to write your own, and the profile settings that do the work 150 characters can't. It's part of our [social media marketing guide](https://brightlyfuture.co.uk/social-media-marketing-guide/).

## What should a business put in its Instagram bio?

Put four things in your bio: what you do and for whom, one reason to trust you, the practical detail customers need, and one call to action. Each gets roughly one short line.

| Line | Its job | Example (vegan bakery) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. What + who | Tells visitors in seconds if you're for them | Vegan sourdough and cakes for Leeds |
| 2. Proof | Gives a reason to choose you | Baked fresh daily since 2019 |
| 3. Practical info | Answers "can I buy from you?" | 📍 Chapel Allerton · Tue–Sun 8–3 |
| 4. Call to action | Says what to do next | 👇 Order a celebration cake |

The bio sits under your name and above your posts, so it's often the first thing a new visitor reads. Your name field, links, category and buttons sit around it and carry the rest (covered below), so the bio text doesn't need to hold everything.

## How do you write your own Instagram bio?

Write your name field first, then the four lines, then check the result on your phone. It takes about 15 minutes.

[SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: Instagram "Edit profile" screen showing the Name, Username, Bio and Links fields. Add manually.]

### Step 1: Put a search keyword in your name field

Your name field is separate from your username, and both are searchable on Instagram. Use it for your business name plus the word people search for, such as "Rosa's | Leeds Vegan Bakery" or "Sam Patel | Bookkeeper". Your username can stay as your brand handle (@rosasbakes).

### Step 2: Write the four lines

Draft each line from the formula above:

1. **What + who.** Use the words your customers use, not your industry's jargon.
2. **Proof.** A real detail: years trading, a certification, a number of clients, a rating. Never invent one.
3. **Practical info.** Location for local businesses, delivery area for shops, availability for services.
4. **Call to action.** One action that matches your first link.

### Step 3: Match the call to action to your buttons and link

If your bio says "Book a table", your first link or action button should take people straight to booking. A bio that says "order online" and a link to your homepage makes visitors hunt for it.

### Step 4: Check it on a phone

Save the bio and view your profile from another account or ask someone to look. Check that the lines break where you meant, that emojis don't replace important words and that the whole bio shows without tapping "more". Bios over a few lines can be cut off on some screens.

## Before and after: fixing a weak bio

Most weak bios describe a feeling instead of the business. Here's an illustration: a typical salon bio, rewritten with the formula.

**Before:**
✨ Living the dream ✨ / Passion for beauty 💕 / Est. 2018 / DM for info

**After:**
Gel nails and nail art in Leeds / Appointment only, Tue–Sat / See prices in Highlights 💅 / 👇 Book your slot

[IMG 03-instagram-bio-before-after.webp | Before and after: rewriting a weak Instagram bio for a nail salon]

What changed:

- **"Living the dream" became the service and the place.** A visitor now knows in a second if this salon is for them.
- **"DM for info" became a clear booking action.** Fewer people message to ask a question they could answer themselves.
- **Prices moved to Highlights.** The bio stays short and the detail is one tap away.

## How do you use the links in your Instagram bio?

You can add up to five links to your Instagram bio. Instagram launched the feature in April 2023, as [Social Media Today reported](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-capacity-to-display-5-links-in-profile-bio/647995/), so you don't need a link-in-bio tool just to share more than one page.

[SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: Instagram "Links" screen in Edit profile showing "Add external link" and a list of links. Add manually.]

Use them in order of importance:

- **Link 1: the action in your bio.** The booking page, shop or sign-up you ask people to use. It's the one most visitors see first.
- **Links 2–3: what people ask for most.** A menu, price list or best-selling product.
- **Leave the rest empty if you don't need them.** Five weak links are harder to choose from than two clear ones.

Add a short title to each link so people know where it goes. If you want to know which link gets clicks, add UTM tags to your website links so the visits show up in your website analytics.

## What else on your profile works with your bio?

Your category, contact buttons, Highlights and pinned posts carry the information that won't fit in 150 characters. Set them up once and update them when something changes.

[SCREENSHOT PLACEHOLDER: a business profile showing the category label under the name and the Call / Email / Book buttons. Add manually.]

- **Category.** Professional accounts can show a label such as "Bakery" or "Accountant" under the name. Pick the closest match so visitors understand your business before reading the bio.
- **Contact and action buttons.** Add the ways customers actually contact you: call, email, directions or a booking button. Then you don't need your phone number in the bio text.
- **Story Highlights.** Use them for prices, FAQs, reviews and how to order. Name them clearly ("Prices", "Reviews") so people find them.
- **Pinned posts.** Pin up to three posts that answer the next question: your best-selling product, a client result or how to book.
- **Branded hashtag.** If customers post about you, add your branded hashtag to the bio so their photos are one tap away. Our guide to [Instagram hashtag strategy for business](https://brightlyfuture.co.uk/instagram-hashtag-strategy-for-business/) explains when one is worth having.

## What Instagram bio mistakes should businesses avoid?

The most common mistake is making visitors guess what you do. These are the others to check for:

- **Writing a slogan instead of a description.** "Dream big ✨" tells visitors nothing about what you sell.
- **Asking people to DM for prices.** Many won't bother. Put prices in a Highlight or on your site.
- **Using several calls to action.** "Follow, shop, book, DM, subscribe" leaves people unsure which to do.
- **Replacing words with emojis.** Screen readers read each emoji's description aloud, so a bio like "🍕 + 🍺 = ❤️" becomes confusing. The University of Kentucky's [accessibility guidance](https://ada.uky.edu/digital-accessibility/social-media) recommends using emojis sparingly and not in place of words.
- **Letting it go out of date.** Old opening hours, finished offers or a broken link cost you customers. Check your bio each time something changes.

## How do you know if your Instagram bio is working?

Compare your profile visits with the actions that follow them in Instagram Insights: link taps, button taps and new follows. If many people visit your profile but few tap or follow, the bio isn't convincing them.

To test a change:

1. **Note your numbers** for profile visits, link taps and follows over two weeks.
2. **Change one line**, such as the proof line or the call to action.
3. **Compare the next two weeks.** Keep the change if more visitors tap or follow.

Look at the proportion, not just the totals. A post that goes viral brings more visits but won't tell you if the bio got better.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should you add pronouns to a business profile?

It's optional. Pronouns suit a personal brand where you are the business, such as a coach or creator. For a business account with a team, they usually aren't needed. Instagram shows them next to your name, and you can choose who sees them.

### How often should you change your Instagram bio?

Update it whenever something it mentions changes, such as hours, offers or your main link. Beyond that, leave it alone long enough to measure, about two weeks per change, so you can tell what worked.

### Should you use your own name or your business name?

Use the name people search for. If customers come to you as a person, as with a hairdresser or coach, lead with your name and add the service. If they know the business, use the business name plus a keyword.

### Can you use the same bio on TikTok and other platforms?

You can reuse the core message, but each platform has its own character limit and layout, so the wording usually needs trimming or expanding. Keep the same description of what you do so people recognise you across platforms.

## Write your bio in four lines

Open your profile now and read your bio as a stranger would. If it doesn't say what you do, who it's for, why to trust you and what to do next, rewrite it with the four lines above, check it on your phone and measure for two weeks.
