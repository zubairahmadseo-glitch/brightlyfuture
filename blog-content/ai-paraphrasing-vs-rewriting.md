# AI Paraphrasing vs Rewriting: What's the Difference?

> **Quick Answer:** Paraphrasing restates one passage in new words while keeping its meaning, order and length. Rewriting rebuilds the whole piece, often with a new structure, angle or audience. In my test, a synonym spinner kept 83% of the original 5-word phrases; a proper paraphrase and a rewrite kept none.

[IMG 01-paraphrasing-featured.webp | AI paraphrasing vs rewriting – the difference tested on one paragraph]

People use "paraphrase" and "rewrite" as if they mean the same thing. So do most AI tools, which put both labels on the same button. The difference matters, though, because it decides whether your text is original, whether you still need a citation, and whether Google sees your page as new or recycled.

I didn't want to explain it with definitions alone. So I took one 64-word paragraph and turned it into three versions: a synonym-spinner output, my own paraphrase and my own rewrite. Then I measured how much of the original each one kept.

## What is the difference between paraphrasing and rewriting?

Paraphrasing changes how a passage is worded. Rewriting changes what the piece is built to do. A paraphrase keeps the original ideas in the same order at roughly the same length. A rewrite can reorder, cut, add examples, change the tone or aim at a different reader, as long as the core message survives.

[IMG 02-paraphrasing-spectrum.webp | Rewording, paraphrasing, rewriting and summarising compared]

| | Paraphrasing | Rewriting |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | A sentence or passage | A section or whole article |
| What changes | Words and sentence shape | Structure, angle, tone, examples |
| Order of ideas | Stays the same | Can change completely |
| Length | Close to the original | Anything; often shorter |
| Main use | Citing sources, clarifying a passage | Updating posts, new audiences, fixing weak drafts |
| Citation needed? | Yes, the idea is still the source's | Yes, if the ideas came from someone else |

Related terms cause the rest of the confusion. Rewording swaps individual words for synonyms and leaves the sentence alone. Summarising keeps only the main points, so it is always shorter than the source. Rephrasing is the loosest of the lot: Scribbr's guide to [paraphrasing, rephrasing and rewording](https://www.scribbr.com/frequently-asked-questions/whats-the-difference-between-paraphrasing-rephrasing-and-rewording/) says it can even reorder whole sentences, which overlaps with rewriting. When a tool says "rephrase", check which one it actually does.

## What happened when I paraphrased and rewrote the same paragraph?

The synonym spinner changed 2 of 64 words and kept 83% of the original's 5-word phrases. My paraphrase kept none of them. My rewrite kept none of them and only 13% of the words in their original order.

Here's the original paragraph I started with:

> "Content marketing works best when every article answers one clear question for one specific reader. Before writing, check what already ranks on Google and note the angle each page takes. Then add something the top results do not have, such as first-hand test data or a free tool. Publishing more posts will not help if each one repeats what readers have already seen."

[IMG 04-paraphrasing-side-by-side.webp | Screenshot: the original, spinner, paraphrase and rewrite side by side with copied phrases highlighted]

- **The spinner** swapped "clear" for "evident" and "help" for "assist". Everything else stayed, word for word.
- **My paraphrase** kept every idea in the same order: one question per reader, check what ranks, add something new, don't repeat. I changed the wording and the shape of each sentence.
- **My rewrite** aimed the same advice at small business owners. It opens with the conclusion ("you don't need more blog posts, you need better ones"), merges two ideas and drops the word "angle" entirely.

[IMG 03-paraphrasing-overlap-test.webp | How much of the original survived: spinner 83%, paraphrase 0%, rewrite 0%]

| Version | Words changed | 5-word phrases copied | Words kept in original order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Synonym spinner | 2 of 64 | 83% | 97% |
| My paraphrase | Most | 0% | 50% |
| My rewrite | Almost all | 0% | 13% |

I measured overlap by comparing word sequences, the same basic idea plagiarism checkers use to match text. The takeaway: a paraphrase can look completely new at the phrase level while still following the source sentence by sentence. That is exactly why it still needs a citation.

## Is paraphrasing considered plagiarism?

Paraphrasing is not plagiarism when you credit the source and genuinely restate the idea in your own words. It becomes plagiarism when you skip the credit, or when you only swap a few words and keep the author's sentence structure.

Scribbr's guidance on [whether paraphrasing is plagiarism](https://www.scribbr.com/frequently-asked-questions/is-paraphrasing-considered-plagiarism/) makes the same point: rearranging an author's words or inserting a few synonyms still counts as plagiarism, even with a citation. My spinner result shows why. With 83% of its 5-word phrases unchanged, that text would match the original in any checker.

Rewriting follows the same rule. If the ideas, research or structure came from someone else, credit them. Changing every word doesn't make an idea yours.

## Can AI paraphrase without plagiarism?

Yes, if the AI changes sentence structure as well as words, you check the output, and you still cite the source of the idea. The risk comes from tools that only swap synonyms, and from people using any tool to hide where a text came from.

Two changes since 2025 matter here:

- **Turnitin now looks for disguised AI text.** On 27 August 2025, Turnitin [launched AI bypasser detection](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/08/27/turnitin-launches-anti-ai-humanizer-feature/), trained on the patterns left by humanizer and rewording tools. Running AI text through a paraphraser is no longer a reliable way to hide it.
- **Google names synonym swapping as spam.** Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), updated in August 2026, list copying content and modifying it "only slightly (for example, by substituting synonyms or using automated techniques)" as scraping.

The safe way to use AI for either job:

1. Feed it your own draft or notes, not someone else's article.
2. Tell it which one you want. "Paraphrase this paragraph, same meaning and length" and "Rewrite this section for beginners, new structure, keep these facts" produce very different results.
3. Read the output against the original. Put back any fact it dropped or changed.
4. Cite every source whose idea you used.

## Does Google penalise paraphrased or rewritten content?

Google doesn't penalise text for being paraphrased, rewritten or AI-assisted. It rates pages low when they add nothing new. The January 2025 update to Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines gives the Lowest rating when almost all main content is "copied or paraphrased with no effort or added value", as [PPC Land's summary of the update](https://ppc.land/google-updates-quality-rater-guidelines-with-ai-content-evaluation-criteria/) quotes.

So the question isn't "paraphrase or rewrite?" It's "what did I add?" For a blog, that usually means a rewrite plus something the source didn't have: your test, your data, your example.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should I paraphrase or quote a source?

Quote when the exact wording matters: a definition, a striking line or a claim you want to show precisely. Paraphrase when only the idea matters and you can say it more simply for your readers. Most blog posts need far more paraphrasing than quoting.

### How do I cite a paraphrase in a blog post?

Name the source in the sentence and link to the original page, for example "Scribbr explains that…" with a link on the source name. Academic styles such as APA also need the author and year in brackets plus a reference list entry.

### Can a paraphrasing tool change the meaning of my text?

Yes. Word-swap tools pick a synonym without reading the sentence, so "the state of the market" can become "the declare of the market", and "not" or a number can get lost in AI rewrites. Check negatives, figures and technical terms line by line before you publish.

### Is it cheating to use a paraphrasing tool at university?

It depends on your institution's policy. Many universities allow tools for language support but treat undisclosed AI rewriting of submitted work as misconduct. Check your course rules and declare the tool if they ask.

## Final thoughts

Paraphrase when you need someone else's idea in your words. Rewrite when your own piece needs a new shape. Either way, the words are the easy part; credit and added value are what keep your content honest and worth ranking.
