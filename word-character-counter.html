5 Tips for Hitting the Right Word Count Without Filler

Quality matters more than quantity. Here is how to write the right amount.

1

Focus on Search Intent, Not Arbitrary Word Counts

Google ranks content that answers the search query completely. A 1,200 word post that fully answers a question will outrank a 3,000 word post filled with irrelevant filler. Use our word counter to track length while keeping every paragraph purposeful.

2

Keep Sentences Short for Better Readability

Average sentence length between 15 and 20 words scores best for readability. Sentences over 25 words become hard to follow. Use our average sentence length metric to identify when your writing gets too complex.

3

Aim for 1% to 2% Keyword Density

SEO experts recommend keeping primary keyword density between 1% and 2%. Going above 3% risks keyword stuffing penalties from Google. Use our free keyword density checker to monitor your keyword usage as you write.

4

Break Long Content into Short Paragraphs

Paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences perform best for online reading. Readers scan content on screens. Large text blocks cause bounce. Use our paragraph counter to make sure your content stays scannable.

5

Use Unique Words for Vocabulary Richness

A high ratio of unique words to total words indicates strong vocabulary. Academic and professional writing should aim for higher diversity. Our unique word counter helps you identify when your writing becomes repetitive.

Characters with spaces counts every character including spaces between words. Characters without spaces only counts letters, numbers, and punctuation marks. Twitter and most social media platforms count characters with spaces. SMS character limits also include spaces.

Reading time is calculated at 238 words per minute, which is the average adult silent reading speed based on research published in the Journal of Memory and Language. We round to the nearest minute for practical display, with a minimum of "less than 1 min" for very short texts.

Speaking time uses 150 words per minute, which is the average conversational speaking pace in English. Professional speakers typically speak at 130 to 170 words per minute. This estimate helps you plan presentations, speeches, podcast scripts, and video narrations.

Keyword density is the percentage of times a specific word or phrase appears in your text relative to the total word count.
