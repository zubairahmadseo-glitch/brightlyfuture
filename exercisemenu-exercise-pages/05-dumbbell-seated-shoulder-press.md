## Quick Answer (AEO)

The seated dumbbell shoulder press is a compound overhead press that trains the front and side delts and the triceps. Sit on an upright bench with back support, start with the dumbbells at shoulder height, palms facing forward, then press them overhead until your arms are straight. Use 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps.

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Anterior Deltoid — the front of the shoulder, lifts the arms overhead.
Lateral Deltoid — the side of the shoulder, assists as the dumbbells press up and out.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Triceps Brachii — straighten the elbows to finish each press.
Upper Trapezius — helps rotate the shoulder blades upward as the arms rise.
Serratus Anterior — rotates and stabilizes the shoulder blades overhead.
Rotator Cuff — keeps the shoulder joint stable.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set the bench upright | Set an adjustable bench to upright or one notch below (about 80–90 degrees). Sit with your back against the pad and feet flat on the floor. |
| 2 | Bring the dumbbells to your shoulders | Rest the dumbbells on your thighs, then kick them up one at a time to shoulder height. Palms face forward and elbows sit slightly in front of your torso. |
| 3 | Brace | Keep your head, upper back, and hips against the pad and tighten your abs so your lower back does not arch away from the bench. |
| 4 | Press overhead | Press the dumbbells straight up until your elbows are straight and the weights are over your shoulders. They can move slightly toward each other at the top. |
| 5 | Lower to shoulder height | Lower the dumbbells under control until they are about level with your ears or a little lower, then press again. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Arching the Lower Back:** Leaning back and arching turns the press into an incline press and stresses the lower back. Keep your hips and upper back on the pad and abs tight.
2. **Elbows Flared Straight Out to the Sides:** Pressing with elbows directly out at 90 degrees can be uncomfortable for the shoulders. Keep them slightly in front of your body.
3. **Cutting the Range Short:** Stopping the dumbbells high above the shoulders reduces the work for the delts. Lower until the dumbbells reach about ear level.
4. **Clanging the Dumbbells Together:** Hitting the weights at the top makes the press less controlled. Bring them close without forcing contact.
5. **Using Momentum:** Bouncing out of the bottom shifts the load away from the shoulders. Press smoothly from a controlled start.

---

## Tips

1. **Use the Back Pad:** The supported back is the main benefit of the seated version. It limits lower back arching and lets you focus on the shoulders.
2. **Get the Dumbbells Up Safely:** Use your thighs to kick each dumbbell up to shoulder height rather than curling heavy weights into position.
3. **Try a Neutral Grip if Your Shoulders Ache:** Pressing with palms facing each other is a common option for lifters who find the palms-forward grip uncomfortable.
4. **Keep Wrists Over Elbows:** Stacking the wrists over the elbows at the bottom gives the most direct pressing line.

## FAQs

1. **Q: Is seated or standing shoulder press better?**
   A: Both build the shoulders. Seated presses support the back and help you focus on the delts, while standing presses demand more trunk stability. A 2013 study by Saeterbakken and Fimland in the Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research found lower posterior deltoid activity in seated presses than standing presses.
2. **Q: How low should the dumbbells go?**
   A: Lower them to about ear level or slightly below, as far as your shoulders allow without pain.
3. **Q: Dumbbell or barbell shoulder press?**
   A: Dumbbells let each arm work independently and allow a more natural wrist position. A barbell usually allows heavier loads.
4. **Q: What bench angle should I use?**
   A: Upright or one notch back from upright. Leaning further back shifts more work to the upper chest.
5. **Q: Is the seated shoulder press good for beginners?**
   A: Yes. The back support makes it one of the easier overhead presses to learn, as long as you start with light dumbbells.

**Citation (for FAQ 1):** Saeterbakken AH, Fimland MS. *Effects of body position and loading modality on muscle activity and strength in shoulder presses.* J Strength Cond Res. 2013;27(7):1824–1831. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23096062/
