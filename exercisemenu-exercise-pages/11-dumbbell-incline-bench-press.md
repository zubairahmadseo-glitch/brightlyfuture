## Quick Answer (AEO)

The incline dumbbell bench press is a compound exercise that trains the upper chest, front shoulders, and triceps. Set a bench to about 30 degrees, start with the dumbbells at your upper chest, then press them up over your shoulders until your arms are straight. Lower them slowly. Use 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps.


## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set the bench angle | Set an adjustable bench to about 30 degrees. Sit with a dumbbell resting on each thigh. |
| 2 | Lie back and position the dumbbells | Lie back and use your thighs to help bring the dumbbells to your upper chest. Plant your feet and pull your shoulder blades back and down. |
| 3 | Set your elbows | Hold the dumbbells with palms facing forward and elbows about 45–70 degrees from your torso, not flared straight out. |
| 4 | Press up | Press the dumbbells up until your arms are straight over your shoulders. They can move slightly toward each other at the top. |
| 5 | Lower with control | Lower the dumbbells over 2–3 seconds until they are beside your upper chest and you feel a stretch across the chest. |
| 6 | Finish safely | After the last rep, bring the dumbbells back to your thighs as you sit up. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Bench Set Too Steep:** Angles above 45 degrees turn the press into more of a shoulder exercise. Keep it near 30 degrees.
2. **Elbows Flared to 90 Degrees:** Fully flared elbows put more stress on the front of the shoulder. Keep them at a moderate angle from your body.
3. **Shoulder Blades Rolling Forward:** Letting the shoulders lift off the bench at the top shifts work away from the chest. Keep the shoulder blades back and down.
4. **Cutting the Bottom Short:** Stopping high reduces the stretch on the upper chest. Lower until the dumbbells are beside your chest.
5. **Dropping the Dumbbells to the Side:** Letting go of heavy dumbbells with your arms out wide can strain the shoulders. Bring them to your thighs first.

---

## Tips

1. **Use About 30 Degrees:** A 2020 study by Rodríguez-Ridao and colleagues found the upper chest was most active at a 30-degree incline, while angles above 45 degrees increased front delt activity.
2. **Keep Your Feet Planted:** Pressing your feet into the floor keeps your body stable on the bench.
3. **Press Up, Not Back Toward Your Face:** The dumbbells should finish over your shoulders, not over your face or stomach.
4. **Start Lighter Than Your Flat Press:** Most lifters use less weight on an incline than on a flat bench.

## FAQs

1. **Q: What is the best incline angle?**
   A: About 30 degrees for most lifters. In the 2020 Rodríguez-Ridao study, higher angles shifted more work to the front delts.
2. **Q: Dumbbell or barbell incline press?**
   A: Dumbbells let each arm work independently and allow a deeper stretch. A barbell lets you use more total weight.
3. **Q: Should the dumbbells touch at the top?**
   A: They can come close, but there is no need to bang them together. Keep the movement controlled.
4. **Q: Why do I feel incline press in my shoulders?**
   A: The bench may be too steep or your elbows may be flared. Lower the angle and keep your elbows at a moderate angle.
5. **Q: Should I do incline or flat press first?**
   A: Do first the press you most want to improve. If upper chest is your priority, start with incline.
