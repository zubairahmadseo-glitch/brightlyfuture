## Quick Answer (AEO)

The one-arm dumbbell row is a unilateral pulling exercise that trains the lats, rhomboids, and middle traps. Brace one hand on a rack or bench, hinge forward with a flat back, and pull the dumbbell toward your hip, keeping the elbow close to your body. Lower it under control. Use 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps per arm.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Latissimus Dorsi — pulls the upper arm back toward the hip.
Rhomboids — pull the shoulder blade toward the spine at the top of the row.
Middle Trapezius — retracts and steadies the shoulder blade.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Posterior Deltoid — assists in pulling the elbow back.
Biceps Brachii and Brachialis — bend the elbow during the pull.
Obliques and Erector Spinae — resist twisting and hold the flat back.
Forearm Flexors — hold the dumbbell.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Brace your free hand | Stand beside a dumbbell rack or bench. Place your non-working hand on it and step back into a staggered stance, with the leg on the working side further back. |
| 2 | Hinge and set your back | Push your hips back until your torso leans well forward. Keep your back flat and your neck in line with your spine. |
| 3 | Let the dumbbell hang | Hold the dumbbell with a neutral grip (palm facing in), arm straight below your shoulder. |
| 4 | Row toward your hip | Pull your elbow up and back, keeping it close to your side, until the dumbbell reaches your lower ribs or hip. |
| 5 | Lower with control | Lower the dumbbell until your arm is straight and you feel a stretch in your lat. Finish all reps, then switch sides. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Twisting the Torso:** Rotating your chest open to lift the dumbbell higher uses momentum and the lower back. Keep your shoulders square to the floor.
2. **Shrugging the Weight Up:** Pulling the shoulder toward the ear moves work to the upper traps. Keep the shoulder down and drive the elbow back.
3. **Rounding the Back:** A rounded back puts the spine in a weak position. Keep a flat back throughout the set.
4. **Short, Jerky Reps:** Yanking the weight up and dropping it fast skips the stretch at the bottom. Lower the dumbbell all the way under control.
5. **Pulling Straight Up to the Chest:** Rowing to the chest with the elbow flared shifts the work toward the upper back and rear delts. Pull toward the hip to bias the lats.

---

## Tips

1. **Think "Elbow to Back Pocket":** This cue keeps the elbow close and the path toward the hip, which keeps the lats working.
2. **Use the Support Hand:** Pressing into the rack or bench keeps your torso steady, so you can use more weight with strict form.
3. **Use Straps When Grip Fails First:** If your grip gives out before your back, straps let you finish the set.
4. **Start with Your Weaker Side:** Do the same number of reps on the stronger side to keep both sides balanced.

## Modifications (Easier/Harder)

| Type | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 🔽 Easier | Bench-Supported Dumbbell Row | Placing one knee and one hand on a flat bench gives more support and makes the flat back easier to hold. |
| 🔽 Easier | Chest-Supported Dumbbell Row | Lying face down on an incline bench removes any need to stabilize the hinge. |
| 🔼 Harder | Meadows Row | Rowing one end of a barbell in a landmine with a staggered stance allows heavy loading and a longer stretch. |
| 🔼 Harder | Unsupported One-Arm Row | Rowing without a hand on a support forces your trunk to resist rotation and adds a balance challenge. |

## FAQs

1. **Q: Should I put my knee on a bench or use a rack?**
   A: A knee on a bench gives the most support, while a hand on a rack with a staggered stance lets you set a longer, more natural pulling path.
2. **Q: Where should I pull the dumbbell to?**
   A: Toward your lower ribs or hip. A path toward the hip with the elbow tucked emphasizes the lats.
3. **Q: Is the one-arm row better than the barbell row?**
   A: It is different. The one-arm row trains each side separately and is easier on the lower back, while the barbell row allows heavier total loads.
4. **Q: Why do I feel it in my biceps?**
   A: The biceps always help, but feeling them most usually means you are pulling with the hand. Focus on driving the elbow back and let the hand act as a hook.
5. **Q: Can beginners do dumbbell rows?**
   A: Yes. With the free hand supported, it is one of the easiest back exercises to learn.
