## Quick Answer (AEO)

The barbell bent-over row is a compound pulling exercise that trains the lats, rhomboids, middle traps, and rear delts. Hinge forward with a flat back, let the bar hang at arm's length, then pull it to your lower ribs or upper stomach and lower it under control. Start with 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps.

---

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Latissimus Dorsi — pulls the upper arms back toward the torso.
Rhomboids — squeeze the shoulder blades together at the top of the row.
Middle Trapezius — retracts the shoulder blades and holds the upper back tight.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Posterior Deltoid — assists in pulling the elbows back.
Biceps Brachii and Brachialis — bend the elbows during the pull.
Erector Spinae — hold the hinged torso position for the whole set.
Hamstrings and Glutes — hold the hips steady in the hinge.
Forearm Flexors — maintain grip on the bar.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pick up the bar | Deadlift the bar to standing with an overhand grip slightly wider than shoulder-width. |
| 2 | Hinge into position | Soften your knees and push your hips back until your torso leans well forward, roughly 45 degrees or lower. Keep your back flat and the bar hanging at arm's length. |
| 3 | Brace your trunk | Take a breath, tighten your abs, and keep your neck in line with your spine, eyes on the floor a little ahead of you. |
| 4 | Row the bar up | Pull your elbows back and up to bring the bar to your lower ribs or upper stomach. Squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top. |
| 5 | Lower under control | Let the bar travel back down until your arms are straight, keeping your torso angle the same on every rep. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Standing Up as You Row:** Letting the torso rise on each rep turns the row into a shrug and shortens the range. Hold the same hinge angle from the first rep to the last.
2. **Rounding the Back:** A rounded lower back under load puts stress on the spine. Keep a flat back and lighten the bar if you cannot hold it.
3. **Jerking the Weight with the Hips:** Using a hip snap to start each rep moves the work away from the back. If you need momentum, the load is too heavy.
4. **Pulling with the Arms Only:** Bending the elbows without moving the shoulder blades leaves the upper back underworked. Think about driving the elbows back, not curling the bar.
5. **Cutting the Bottom Short:** Stopping before the arms are straight removes the stretch on the lats. Lower until your arms are fully extended.

---

## Tips

1. **Pull to Where the Bar Naturally Lands:** Most lifters touch the bar to the lower chest or upper stomach. Pick one spot and use it on every rep so the lift stays consistent.
2. **Keep the Bar Close to Your Legs:** A bar that drifts forward increases the pull on your lower back. Let it travel straight up and down beneath your shoulders.
3. **Change Grip to Shift the Focus:** An overhand grip with elbows slightly out emphasizes the upper back. An underhand grip with elbows tucked brings in more lats and biceps.
4. **Use Straps When Grip Fails First:** If your grip gives out before your back, wrist straps let you finish the set with good form.

## Modifications (Easier/Harder)

| Type | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 🔽 Easier | Chest-Supported Dumbbell Row | Lying face down on an incline bench removes the need to hold the hinge, so beginners and anyone with lower back fatigue can focus on the pull. |
| 🔽 Easier | Seated Cable Row | The seat and foot plate support your body, and the cable lets you adjust the load in small steps. |
| 🔼 Harder | Pendlay Row | Each rep starts from the floor with the torso parallel to the ground, which requires more upper back power and a stricter hinge. |
| 🔼 Harder | Paused Barbell Row | Holding the bar against your body for 1–2 seconds at the top removes momentum and makes the upper back work harder. |

## FAQs

1. **Q: Should I use an overhand or underhand grip?**
   A: Both work. An overhand grip is more common and biases the upper back, while an underhand grip lets the elbows stay closer to the body and involves more lats and biceps.
2. **Q: How bent over should I be?**
   A: A torso angle between about 45 degrees and parallel to the floor is typical. The more horizontal you are, the more your back works to hold the position.
3. **Q: Is the barbell row bad for the lower back?**
   A: Not when performed with a flat back and a load you can control. If your lower back tires before your upper back, try a chest-supported row instead.
4. **Q: Bent-over row or lat pulldown — which is better?**
   A: They train the back in different directions. The row is a horizontal pull, and the pulldown is a vertical pull, so most programs include one of each.
5. **Q: Can beginners do barbell rows?**
   A: Yes, with light loads once they can hold a flat-back hip hinge. Beginners who struggle with the hinge can start with a chest-supported or cable row.
