## Quick Answer (AEO)

The close-grip bench press is a compound pressing lift that targets the triceps while also working the chest and front shoulders. Lie on a flat bench, grip the bar about shoulder-width apart, lower it to your lower chest with elbows tucked, then press back to lockout. Use 3 to 4 sets of 6 to 10 reps.

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Triceps Brachii — extends the elbows and does more of the work than in a standard-grip bench press.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Pectoralis Major — still contributes to pressing the bar up from the chest.
Anterior Deltoid — assists the press at the shoulder.
Serratus Anterior — helps stabilize the shoulder blades.
Forearm Flexors and Extensors — keep the wrists stable under the bar.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set up on the bench | Lie on a flat bench with your eyes under the bar, feet flat on the floor, and shoulder blades pulled back and down. |
| 2 | Take a shoulder-width grip | Grip the bar with hands about shoulder-width apart, wrists stacked over your forearms. |
| 3 | Unrack and hold over the shoulders | Lift the bar out of the rack and hold it with arms straight above your shoulders. |
| 4 | Lower with elbows tucked | Lower the bar under control to your lower chest, keeping your elbows close to your sides rather than flared out. |
| 5 | Press to lockout | Press the bar back up until your elbows are straight, keeping your back and glutes on the bench. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Gripping Too Narrow:** Hands touching or a few inches apart bend the wrists back and strain the elbows. A shoulder-width grip still loads the triceps without that stress.
2. **Flaring the Elbows:** Letting the elbows drift wide moves the work back toward the chest and shoulders. Keep them tucked close to your torso as the bar lowers.
3. **Lowering to the Upper Chest:** With a narrow grip and tucked elbows, the bar naturally lands lower on the chest. Lowering too high can strain the shoulders.
4. **Bouncing off the Chest:** Rebounding the bar uses momentum instead of muscle. Touch the chest lightly and press.
5. **Bent Wrists:** Wrists folded backward under load irritate the joint. Keep the bar over your forearms.

---

## Tips

1. **Use It as a Triceps Strength Builder:** The close grip lets you load the triceps more heavily than most isolation exercises, so it works well early in a push or upper-body session.
2. **Keep Safety Pins or a Spotter:** As with any barbell bench press, set safety arms just below chest height or train with a spotter.
3. **Squeeze the Bar Hard:** A firm grip helps keep the wrists straight and the bar steady.
4. **Keep the Same Setup as Your Bench Press:** The shoulder blade position, foot placement, and brace stay the same. Only the grip width and elbow path change.

## Modifications (Easier/Harder)

| Type | Title | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 🔽 Easier | Close-Grip Push-Up | Placing your hands under your shoulders with elbows tucked trains the same pattern with body weight. |
| 🔽 Easier | Smith Machine Close-Grip Bench Press | The fixed bar path removes the balancing demand so you can focus on elbow position. |
| 🔼 Harder | Paused Close-Grip Bench Press | Pausing for 1–2 seconds on the chest removes the stretch reflex and makes the press off the chest harder. |
| 🔼 Harder | Close-Grip Floor Press | Pressing from the floor stops each rep when the upper arms touch the ground, making the triceps-heavy top half of the press do most of the work. |

## FAQs

1. **Q: How close should my grip be?**
   A: About shoulder-width is enough for most lifters. Going much narrower adds wrist and elbow stress without a clear benefit.
2. **Q: Is the close-grip bench press a chest or triceps exercise?**
   A: Both, but it shifts more work to the triceps than the standard bench press. The chest and front delts still help press the bar.
3. **Q: Will it improve my regular bench press?**
   A: It can, because it strengthens the triceps, which help lock out the bar. Many strength programs use it as a bench press assistance lift.
4. **Q: Can I use an EZ bar instead?**
   A: Yes. An EZ bar's angled grip can feel easier on the wrists for some lifters, though it is harder to rack on some benches.
5. **Q: How much weight can I expect to lift?**
   A: Most lifters handle less weight than on their regular bench press because the narrower grip reduces chest involvement. Start light and build up.
