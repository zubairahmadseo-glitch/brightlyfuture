## Quick Answer (AEO)

The barbell hip thrust is a hip extension exercise that targets the glutes. Rest your upper back on a bench with a padded bar across your hips, plant your feet, then drive your hips up until your torso and thighs form a straight line. Squeeze, then lower. Use 3 to 4 sets of 8 to 12 reps.

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Gluteus Maximus — extends the hips to lift the bar and is under the most tension at the top.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Hamstrings — assist hip extension.
Adductor Magnus — assists hip extension.
Quadriceps — help hold the knees at about 90 degrees at the top.
Gluteus Medius — helps keep the knees from caving in.
Abdominals — keep the ribs down and the pelvis from tilting.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set up against the bench | Sit on the floor with the lower edge of your shoulder blades against the side of a flat bench. Roll a padded barbell over your legs until it sits in the crease of your hips. |
| 2 | Plant your feet | Bend your knees and place your feet flat, about hip-width apart. Hold the bar with both hands to keep it steady. |
| 3 | Brace and tuck your chin | Tighten your abs and keep your chin slightly tucked so your eyes look forward, not up at the ceiling. |
| 4 | Drive your hips up | Push through your heels and mid-foot to lift your hips until your torso is parallel to the floor and your shins are about vertical. |
| 5 | Squeeze at the top | Hold for one second, squeezing your glutes without arching your lower back. |
| 6 | Lower with control | Lower your hips toward the floor under control, then start the next rep. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Arching the Lower Back at the Top:** Overextending the spine to finish the rep moves work away from the glutes. Keep your ribs down and stop when your hips are fully extended.
2. **Feet Too Far or Too Close:** Feet too far forward shift work to the hamstrings, and too close shift work to the quads. Set them so your shins are vertical at the top.
3. **Pushing Through the Toes:** Lifting the heels takes the glutes out of the movement. Drive through your heels and mid-foot.
4. **Bench Too High or Too Low:** A bench that hits the neck or the middle of the back makes the setup unstable. The bench edge should sit just below your shoulder blades.
5. **Skipping the Pad:** A loaded bar on the hip bones is painful and limits how much you can lift. Use a bar pad or a folded mat.

---

## Tips

1. **Keep Your Chin Tucked:** Looking forward rather than up helps keep the ribs down and the lower back from arching.
2. **Pause at Lockout:** A one-second hold at the top makes sure the glutes, not momentum, finish each rep.
3. **Use Bigger Plates When Possible:** Standard 45 cm (17.7 in) plates raise the bar high enough to roll over your legs easily.
4. **Program It with a Squat or Hinge:** A 2023 Frontiers in Physiology study by Plotkin and colleagues found that 9 weeks of hip thrusts and back squats produced similar glute growth, while squats grew the quads and adductors more. Each lift has its own role.

## FAQs

1. **Q: Is the hip thrust better than squats for glutes?**
   A: Not clearly. A 2023 study by Plotkin and colleagues found similar glute growth from both. Hip thrusts are useful because they load the glutes without much knee bend.
2. **Q: Where should the bar sit?**
   A: In the crease of your hips, below the hip bones, with a pad. Too high on the stomach or too low on the thighs makes the lift unstable.
3. **Q: Why do I feel hip thrusts in my hamstrings?**
   A: Your feet are probably too far from your body. Move them closer until your shins are about vertical at the top.
4. **Q: Why do I feel it in my quads?**
   A: Your feet are likely too close to your hips. Move them a little further away.
5. **Q: How heavy can hip thrusts go?**
   A: Many lifters can hip thrust more weight than they squat because the range of motion is shorter and the back is supported. Build up gradually with good lockout.

**Citation (for Tip 4 & FAQ 1):** Plotkin DL, et al. *Hip thrust and back squat training elicit similar gluteus muscle hypertrophy and transfer similarly to the deadlift.* Front Physiol. 2023;14:1279170. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2023.1279170/full
