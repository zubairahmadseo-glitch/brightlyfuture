## Quick Answer (AEO)

The dumbbell lateral raise is an isolation exercise for the side deltoids. Stand holding light dumbbells at your sides, keep a slight bend in your elbows, and raise your arms out to the sides until they reach shoulder height. Lower them slowly. Use 3 to 4 sets of 10 to 20 reps with a weight you can control.

## Muscles Worked

**Primary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Lateral Deltoid — the side of the shoulder, lifts the arm out to the side.
```

**Secondary Muscles (one per line)**
```
Anterior Deltoid — assists, especially if the arms drift forward.
Supraspinatus — a rotator cuff muscle that helps start the raise.
Upper Trapezius — helps rotate the shoulder blade upward near the top.
Serratus Anterior — rotates and stabilizes the shoulder blade.
```

## How-To Steps

| # | Step Title | Step Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stand tall | Stand with feet hip-width apart, holding a dumbbell in each hand at your sides. Brace your abs and keep your chest up. |
| 2 | Set your elbows | Keep a slight bend in your elbows and hold it for the whole set. |
| 3 | Raise out to the sides | Lift the dumbbells out to your sides in a wide arc, leading with your elbows. |
| 4 | Stop at shoulder height | Stop when your arms are about parallel to the floor, with palms facing down. |
| 5 | Lower slowly | Lower the dumbbells back to your sides over about 2 seconds without letting them drop. |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Using Too Much Weight:** Heavy dumbbells force you to swing and shrug. The side delts are small, so use a weight you can lift and lower under control.
2. **Swinging the Body:** Rocking the torso to start each rep uses momentum instead of the shoulders. Keep your body still.
3. **Shrugging the Shoulders:** Lifting the shoulders toward the ears shifts the work to the upper traps. Keep your shoulders down as you raise your arms.
4. **Lifting Too High Without Control:** Raising well above shoulder height with a heavy weight often turns into a shrug. Stop around shoulder height unless you can go higher with control.
5. **Locked or Deeply Bent Elbows:** Locked elbows strain the joint, and a deep bend shortens the lever and makes the exercise too easy. Keep a slight, fixed bend.

---

## Tips

1. **Lead with the Elbows:** Thinking about pushing your elbows out to the walls keeps the side delts working.
2. **Raise Slightly in Front of the Body:** Moving the arms a little forward of straight out to the side (the line of the shoulder blade) often feels more comfortable for the shoulders.
3. **Use Higher Reps:** Light weights for 12–20 reps are common for this exercise because it is hard to control heavy loads.
4. **Try It Seated:** Sitting on a bench reduces body swing if you struggle to keep your torso still.

## FAQs

1. **Q: How heavy should lateral raises be?**
   A: Light enough to raise to shoulder height without swinging or shrugging, usually far less than you use for presses.
2. **Q: Should my thumbs point up or down?**
   A: Keep your palms facing down or your hands level. Turning the thumbs down ("pouring the jug") can irritate the shoulder in some people.
3. **Q: Do I need lateral raises if I do shoulder presses?**
   A: Presses mainly load the front delts. Lateral raises add direct work for the side delts, which build shoulder width.
4. **Q: How high should I raise the dumbbells?**
   A: About shoulder height. You can go a little higher if you can keep your shoulders down and the movement controlled.
5. **Q: Can I do lateral raises every day?**
   A: There is no need. Two to three sessions per week with rest days in between is enough for most lifters.
